# THE ONE PAGE

The woods went quiet when the world got finished. Everything out past the treeline is believed in
by something, and belief is what lets a thing act. The makers fix belief into an animal by hand,
with a die and a punch, and leave a tin token in the fur. Whatever wears one has been seen, and
cannot be talked out of what it is.

Sight is belief, and belief runs out with age. Small children see everything and cannot be sent. To
an adult the ridge at night is empty woods. The only people who can both see and walk all night are
teenagers, and every one of them is a season from losing it.

Camp Hearthwood closed thirty years ago, after it lost eleven kids in one night. This week the
thing in the woods reached the road, and twenty people walked up the camp's two-track toward the
only light on the ridge: a burn crew off the ridge, families out of cars left at the gate with the
doors open, and two locals who knew the place was empty. Nine of the twenty can see. Four can be
sent. Four small children are at the fire. Nobody is staff and nobody appointed anyone.

You carry the tool you came in with, and it gets stronger because you have never once doubted it,
which is the makers' trick run without a licence. The camp banks the tin you cut off what you
killed, read out at the fire as one number that only goes up. The price is your eyes: the more
certain you are of your kit, the less the woods show you.

You go out because there is no tin at camp, and because the youngest child is further out at the
fence every week.

# CHAPTER ONE: THE SANCTUARY AND THE CAST

## 1. The recommendation, first

**The camp is a hamlet of twenty people who walked toward the only light on the ridge, and the
reason it is a game is that nine of them can see and only four of those nine can be sent.**

The other eleven have crossed the threshold. Four of the nine who can see are small children who
cannot be walked anywhere, and one is thirteen and too small to carry a pack. That leaves four, and
the four are the playable cast, aged 16 to 19. The people who give the orders are 20 to 24 and have
just lost their sight or are losing it this season, and they know. That distribution is not new
lore: it is the spine's age bands (`STORY_DESIGN.md:15-26`) turned into a population, and it hands
the sanctuary its entire power structure for free.

There is a tenth person who can see, and nobody at camp knows it. That is Brock, and section 7 is
about him.

The four are two seasonal crew and two locals, and they arrived by different roads in the same
week:

| | Name | Age | Was | Tool carried in | Play identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **DEZ** | 19 | Conservation corps crew, kept the daily log | A rock bar (five feet of steel, seventeen pounds) | Swung heavy melee, commitment, breaks rather than cuts |
| 2 | **JUNIOR** | 16 | Ran her father's trapline on the state land | A coil of snare cable, pliers, stakes | Placed and persistent, prediction and terrain |
| 3 | **VIC** | 17 | Shone deer at night off the county road, illegally | A 12-volt spotlight on a battery in a pack frame | Held beam, single target, metered, draws attention |
| 4 | **HOLLIS** | 18 | Prescribed burn crew, one season in | A drip torch | Sustained ground fire, denial, terrain shaping |

Max, Junie and Gus are cut, all three, and section 10 says why in their own words.

Brock stays, keeps the name Brock, loses the species and the rank. He becomes the camp's caretaker:
the last employee of a dead institution, forty-one years old and read by everyone at camp as sixty.

## 2. The sanctuary

### 2.1 What the place is now

The sign at the gate still says CAMP HEARTHWOOD. Nobody uses the name. On the radio and at the fire
it is "up at the camp," and the survivors' habit of not saying the name is the first thing that
tells you the institution is dead. Keep Hearthwood on the sign: it is already our invention rather
than anybody's property (`AUTHENTICITY.md:1320` names Camp Hearthwood, Troop 12 and Troop 13 as
ours), and it costs nothing.

**Scale is locked at hamlet, and it is locked by the code.** The camp is 1560 by 1080 world units,
which `AUTHENTICITY.md:388-391` rules is "ONE TROOP CAMPSITE, not a summer camp." The library's
settlement-scale table (`research/communities-premises/anatomy-believable-community/report.md` sec
5) puts that at hamlet: population roughly 10 to 50, anonymity none, no resident authority, a
stranger is an event, and, in its own words, this is the scale where the social logic of a horror
or deception game lives. That is a gift. No institution has to be invented, no privacy has to be
simulated, and every scale-mismatch red flag in that table is out of reach by construction.

**Twenty people.** Four children (5 to 9), one thirteen-year-old, four in the window (16 to 19),
five young adults (20 to 24), six adults (25 and up). The number is countable, it is written down,
and it can go down. Per `research/communities-premises/material-stakes-grounding/report.md` sec 5,
material beats big: twenty with names is a stake, "the survivors" is not.

Where the number lives matters, and canon constrains it twice.
`AUTHENTICITY.md:1199-1213` caps the counted-absence objects at three (buddy board, duty roster,
quartermaster's tags) and says outright: "Three is the number. Do not add a fourth." So the head
count gets no object of its own. It goes onto the duty roster board, which already hangs on a nail
at the gateway with a pencil on a string (`AUTHENTICITY.md:1139`, `AUTHENTICITY.md:1210`), and it
goes on **over the top of the filled columns**, which is the additive-layer mechanic all three
counted objects share (`AUTHENTICITY.md:1201-1202`). **The last three columns stay blank.** Those
blanks are the object's entire payoff, the thing that tells the player the exact meal the camp
stopped (`AUTHENTICITY.md:432`, `AUTHENTICITY.md:1210`), and filling them deletes it.

One more constraint, and it is a hard one: at camp scale the roster board is about 40 by 30 units
and handwriting on it is not paintable, so canon sends the handwriting to the find card
(`AUTHENTICITY.md:1096`). So the head count in two hands, crossed out and rewritten smaller, is
**find-card text, not painted pixels.** What the board shows at camp scale is a grid with a dark
smear over the filled end and three clean columns at the right.

### 2.2 Three arrival vectors, one week

The spine dates the catalyst precisely: patience stopped being an option, they began Signing
everything that moves at once, and "this week, it reached the road" (`STORY_DESIGN.md:39-44`).

1. **From above.** A seasonal crew was at a spike camp on the ridge, cutting line for a burn on the
   sand plain. Seven went up. Four came down the burn line toward the only light they could see.
   Hollis and Dez are two of the four; Roz and the sawyer are the other two.
2. **From the road.** Cars. The forest reached the road, and people walked up the camp's access
   two-track because it went away from the road. **Their cars are still at the gate with the doors
   open.** This is the camp's best piece of residue and it is nearly free:
   `research/communities-premises/lived-in-before-you-arrived-design/report.md` sec 3.1 and 3.2 rate
   absence residue as a magic trick and require it to be datable, and open car doors with a week of
   pine litter on the seats date themselves.
3. **The locals.** Individuals who were already out there for their own reasons and who knew the
   camp was empty, which nobody from away did. Vic and Junior came in alone, two days apart, and
   the camp's regard for each of them is different from its regard for the crew.

Two groups plus two individuals is deliberate.
`research/communities-premises/why-player-gets-the-shop-premise-catalog/report.md` sec 1 names
"cast begins as strangers" as a null-premise symptom on the RELATIONSHIP channel. Nobody in this
camp begins as a stranger to everybody.

### 2.3 What holds them together, and what the days are

Three things hold it, and none of them is loyalty:

- **The children.** You cannot walk four small kids down a road that is not a road any more. This
  is the material floor under everything, and it is why the camp does not disperse.
- **The fire.** The adults keep it big because they cannot see and light is all they have. The
  teenagers know what a fire on a ridge looks like from the treeline. That argument runs every
  night and cannot be won, because winning it means telling eleven people why. It is the same
  object as the shipped finding that **they are burning the camp**
  (`AUTHENTICITY.md:569-578`): the wood coming to the fire is the paddle rack, the interpretive
  signs, the fence rails and the bulletin board, because firewood needs a saw, an axe, somebody who
  can drop a tree, and a wood lot, and these people have none of those.
- **Brock.** He knows where the water is, which building holds, and what not to do after dark. He
  is right every time, and nobody has worked out why.

Daily reality, deliberately unglamorous: water is carried, not piped. **The thirty-year-old kitchen
holds no food.** The derelict law puts food in the gone-or-nearly tier alongside paper and canvas
(`AUTHENTICITY.md:484-548`, and `AUTHENTICITY.md:491` for food specifically), so what survived in
there is metal, stone, glass and enamel: pots, a griddle, jars, no calories. Food is car trunks
plus whatever Junior brings in, which is the only fresh protein in camp and is the reason people
tolerate her. Latrine discipline did not exist in week one and is becoming a real problem in week
two. Nobody's phone works. One truck battery still turns a radio over.

### 2.4 Two hubs, filtered differently, and they are not in the same place

`research/communities-premises/gossip-networks-informal-power/report.md` sec 2 rules that a gossip
hub is never a place designed for gossip: it is a place designed for something else where gossip
accumulates as a byproduct of foot traffic, and that a settlement needs two or more hubs with
genuinely different filters (its sec 11.4 prototype tier). Two, and that is all:

- **The water point.** Rationed, on a rota, and everyone must pass through it. Slow, reliable. It
  is where you learn who is sick, who is hoarding, and who did not show for their turn. This is the
  report's own gritty-survival worked line, and it lands here without modification.
- **The truck.** Fast, unreliable. News from away, rumour, and who is thinking about leaving.
  Whoever sits in that cab holds the FIRST-TO-KNOW mechanism, whose power the report ties to
  infrastructure and a shelf life, and here the infrastructure is a car battery that is dying.

**The truck is not at the gate, and that matters.** The gateway already holds the duty roster
(`AUTHENTICITY.md:1210`), the entrance, and the arrivals' cars. On a camp that is one troop
campsite, stacking the fast rumour hub there too would collapse the two-hub requirement into one
place where everything happens. So: **the truck sits partway down the two-track, below the gate,
where it stopped.** It is the same truck the three who left walked away from, with their note still
under the wiper and the radio running off its battery: one object doing three jobs (the departure
residue, the radio, the hub) and out of earshot of the gate.

**That battery is the camp's material spine.** One resource, out of the cars, is simultaneously the
radio's power, the camp's only electric light, and Vic's ammunition. It is countable, contested by
named people, and it depletes. `research/narrative/mechanics-as-metaphor/report.md` sec 3.6 asks
for a cost tied to a visible consequence the player watches accumulate, and a row of dead batteries
by the gate is that, with no dialogue attached.

**Ruling, because chapter two owns the currency and this would otherwise collide with it: the
battery is not a currency.** It is a camp-side scarcity with named claimants and a diegetic meter
on Vic's tool. It is never spent at a shop, never converts, and never appears in the same sentence
as tin. Open call 8 asks the creator to confirm that, because it is the one place where this
chapter could quietly annex the economy chapter's job.

### 2.5 Three processes, and the one that cannot be stopped

`research/communities-premises/lived-in-before-you-arrived-design/report.md` sec 2.4 sets the
minimum viable lived-in set: at least one process the player interrupts, one that already resolved
without them, and one that keeps moving after them, entered at stage 3 of 5 (its sec 2.2, late
enough that history shows, early enough that intervention matters). For a roguelite,
`anatomy-believable-community/report.md` sec 3 says to put the clock on the run counter: the
dungeon resets, the town accrues.

1. **Interruptible, stage 3: they are eating the camp.** Per `AUTHENTICITY.md:1142-1143`, the first
   build the player chooses is not the dock, it is deciding to stop burning the camp, and as they
   build, the missing paddle rack rails come back. That is the camp progression's real subject and
   it already exists on paper.
2. **Already resolved, stage 5: three people left.** In week one, three walked down the two-track
   for the road and did not come back, and the camp settled its argument about leaving before the
   player arrived. Residue: the truck below the gate with the doors open, none of their things in
   it, and a note under a wiper that the camp has stopped reading. Nobody reopens the question. The
   question being closed is worse than the question being live.
3. **Unstoppable, on the run counter: the children are getting closer to the treeline.** They
   believe totally, so they see everything (`STORY_DESIGN.md:19-20`), and what they see interests
   them. Every few nights the youngest is a little further out at the fence line, and her drawings
   are a little more detailed, and they are accurate about things nobody else has seen yet. This is
   the camp's dread clock, it needs no meter, and it is the correct answer to the horror report's
   requirement that the writing re-inject uncertainty at the same rate the systems inject
   competence (`research/narrative/horror-writing/report.md` sec 7.3).

Her drawings pin to the cabin pinboard, which already exists as a spot (`js/camp.js:832`) `[code]`.
They are not the field guide's child's-handwriting entry: that one is a Troop 12 plant and stays
where it is.

### 2.6 One line of residue I want to spend on Brock

`AUTHENTICITY.md:564-567` calls this the best detail in its section, and it currently has no owner:
the huddle of shelters has migrated inside the fire's spark radius, and one shelter sits apart from

**It is Brock's, and he does not sleep in the cabin.** Thirty years of habit, pitched the way the

**The honoring lines** (its sec 9 requires one per premise, naming the system that collects):

- The obligation is enforced by **the head count on the duty roster**, re-read on arrival every
  night, and it can be smaller than it was.
- The complicity is enforced by **the record**, which is the progression screen: every permanent
  upgrade is bought by adding a token to the archive, and the archive is worn.

**Delete-the-premise test** (its sec 10, and the test is stated properly in chapter two, section 6,
because that is where it was previously stated backwards): delete this sanctuary and the game loses
its progression currency, its unlock gates, its difficulty dial (which of four people you send, and
who is left at camp if they do not come back), and its clock. It does not lose its combat. That is
the correct ratio.

**Day-one community position** (its sec 5, which requires that you can write the community's
unspoken line before the player acts). Here it is, and it is spoken by nobody:

> *You can still see it. So you are going out, and none of us are going to say what that means
> where the children can hear.*

## 4. The cast: four, and why

**Recommendation: four playable, authored in two waves of two.**

1. **The combat ruling makes the roster the variety budget.** With no weapon slots and no weapon
   pickups, the tool no longer comes out of the card pool: character choice becomes the largest
   single build decision in the game. `research/game-design/game-meta-features-systems/report.md`
   sec 2.4 (`:527`) rules that healthy roguelite meta-progression unlocks options, not strength.
   Once tools are people, people are the options. Three tools is a thin permanent variety budget
   for a game that intends to be replayed.
2. **The fourth identity does not exist in the code today.** Placed-and-waiting is absent from the
   entire weapon table. The nearest thing is the canteen's three-second puddle
   (`js/data.js:118-127`) `[code]`, which is auto-fired and expires rather than placed and armed.
   Cutting to three means cutting the only genuinely new playstyle, and the three that remain map
   onto the shipped hatchet (`js/data.js:63`) and roaster (`js/data.js:52`), which means the
   reboot's combat would be a reskin.
3. **Four gives the sanctuary a fault line.** Two crew and two locals is 2 against 2 with real
   content on both sides: the crew's paperwork helped finish the map that starved the woods, and
   the locals were out at night doing things they should not have been. Each side is right about
   the other. Three people cannot carry a fault line without one of them being a tiebreak.
4. **The cost is honest and it is per-character, not marginal.** A fourth character is S of
   plumbing (three appended table rows at `js/data.js:12-37`, `js/data.js:495-505`,
   `js/roster.js:8-20`, plus the hardcoded-to-three sites listed in chapter three) and L of
   authoring: a portrait base plus four or five hand-painted faces in a registry whose existing
   entries run 86 to 278 lines (`js/portraits.js:696-781` Max at 86,
   `js/portraits.js:791-1068` Junie at 278) `[code]`, five sprite sites, **36 barks, and 37
   rendered voice clips** (`tools/voice/lines.json` holds exactly 37 rows for each of max, junie
   and gus, being 36 barks plus one select line, and `assets/voice/en/max`, `/junie` and `/gus`
   each hold 37 `.webm` files) `[code]`. A new character also inherits none of the shared
   `scoutTorso` helper (`js/portraits.js:327`) that lets Max cost 86 lines, which is why a new
   portrait lands at the 230 to 280 end. Every character costs that. Four characters is four L, not
   three L and a bolt-on, so the number should be chosen on design merit and then paid for.
5. **Two waves.** Dez and Junior first, because they are the two most mechanically distinct and the
   two whose registers are furthest apart; Vic and Hollis second. The wave-one pair proves the
   eerie-adult register in a shipping build before the art bill doubles. There is no automated gate
   for "this portrait looks cheap," so the portrait and sprite work is quality-model work
   regardless of who writes the tables.

**Protagonist type, decided once for the whole roster**
(`research/narrative/writing-the-player-protagonist/report.md` sec 2 and sec 12.1 require one
decision for the roster, not one per character): **voiced and pre-defined.** Full human voice
acting is the target and the game already characterises through barks, so hybrid's cost (every beat
must hold across authored variance) buys nothing here. Projection comes from choosing which of four
people to send, not from leaving a temperament open. The one axis left open is not personality: it
is **whether they are right**, and the game never settles it.

Per-character role identity is authored in full (situation, stakes, relationships, competence, and
what others say about them) and grounded in verbs, per its sec 4. The deliverable that keeps them
consistent is the consistency anchor: five to eight lines that are the character plus two or three
negative controls that are almost right, which
`research/narrative/character-writing-arcs/report.md` sec 5.3 calls the single highest-leverage
onboarding artifact. One quotable line and one negative control per character are below; the full
anchors are the next unit, size M for the roster.

## 5. The four

### 5.1 DEZ, 19. The rock bar.

**Before.** City kid on a conservation corps crew for the money, because the alternative was worse.
Had never slept outside before June. He is not the strongest person on the crew and nobody believes
that when they look at him: the bar works because it is seventeen pounds of steel and gravity. What
he actually is is the crew's record: he kept the daily log, he took the photographs for the state,
and he can tell you the date of everything that has happened since the ridge went bad.

**Why he was out there.** Prying rock for a trail drain on the last unfinished stretch of line. His
crew was part of the hands that finished the map (`STORY_DESIGN.md:39-44`).

**Wants.** Everyone's name written down right, and the record to get out.

**Wrong about.** He believes that if it is documented, somebody comes. Nobody is coming. Worse:
under the spine, the record is what starved them. He is writing the thing that makes them real, and
he will not stop, because stopping is worse than being complicit. This is
`STORY_DESIGN.md:32-37` (the progression system is the horror, the Badge Board is complicity, not
power) relocated from a design note into a person.

**Contradiction.** The one person here who insists on accuracy is the one whose accuracy is the
weapon being used against everybody, and he knows, and he keeps writing.

**Fear.** He gets louder and more organised. He counts out loud: rooms, doors, seconds, people.
Late at night he writes himself into the log in the third person.

**Tool: the rock bar.** Five feet, seventeen pounds. It does not cut. It breaks: armour, stance,
legs, and props. Slow committed swings and drops, real recovery frames, and it levers things in the
world (a stump, a stone, a jammed door).

**Grows into.** Nothing fantastical, ever. **The bar is never sharpened.** It gets heavier: the
swing shakes the ground, then drives things into the dirt, then leaves the arena changed where it
landed. What grows forever on Dez is the man's willingness, and the tool is the same object in the
last hour as in the first.

**Flagship, derived from build.** His reads as what the ground does when the bar lands. Item chapter
owns the derivation.

**Fan art hook.** The bar across both shoulders with his wrists hooked over it, and a wire-bound
notebook in his back pocket with the pencil through the spiral.

**Quotable line.** "Say your name again. Slower."

**Negative control (almost right, wrong).** "We're going to be okay." He never reassures.
`research/narrative/horror-writing/report.md` sec 7.2 names dialogue that reassures the player as a
writing-only leak with no mechanical fix, and Dez is where that leak would enter this game.

**Recommend he is the free starter,** and that the cold open is his night. The first voice in the
game is the record-keeper's, which frames the whole thing as his log. This also has a code
consequence, and it is in chapter three's cost table: the free starter's id is the fallback
constant in three places, and all three move in the same commit.

### 5.2 JUNIOR, 16. The snare wire.

**Before.** Runs her father's trapline on the state land behind the county road. Her father is
alive, at home, and not able to run it, and the cheques from the fur buyer are household income.
The licence is his, so the slips are signed in his name, in her handwriting, which is a small crime
she has been committing since she was fourteen. Everyone calls her Junior because that is the name
on the paperwork.

**Why she was out there.** Checking sets at first light. Her line has been coming up wrong for a
week: animals dead and not eaten, animals out of sets that cannot be gotten out of, and one set
holding a piece of tin and no animal. **She held a Sign in the dark for about ten seconds, thought
it was a tag off somebody else's line, and left it hanging where it was.** It is still out there.
She is the first person in the county to have touched one, and she does not have one.

That last clause is load-bearing and it is not a taste call. Brock's act two ask is "Bring me one,
if you can pry it loose. I have wanted to look at one up close for thirty years. I have also, and
I cannot stress this enough, been too much of a coward" (`js/dialogue.js:232`) `[code]`, and
`STORY_DESIGN.md:807-810` makes that line the twist's structural proof: a lie the player cannot
detect that is already in the build. A playable character who walks into camp holding a Sign voids
the ask on night one and takes the cover story's job away. (`STORY_DESIGN.md:809` cites
`js/dialogue.js:199-203` for that line, which is stale: it lives at `:232`.)

**Wants.** To keep the line running. She is the only person in camp whose want points back out into
the woods, which makes her the one who volunteers, which is the cleanest possible motive for a
roguelite night.

**Wrong about.** She thinks this is a season. Something you get through, like a bad winter or a bad
fur market. She is treating an apocalypse as weather, and everything she knows about getting
through a bad winter is what makes her wrong.

**Contradiction.** She is the gentlest person in the camp with people and completely matter of fact
about killing animals: she will hold a child's hand and then dispatch something in front of her
without her face changing. The camp finds her frightening and cannot say why. The deeper one: a
Sign is a piece of tin punched into fur by somebody who wants to own what it marks, and so is a
trapper's tag. Methodologically she is on the same side as the thing hunting them, and she has
worked that out.

**Fear.** She gets busy. She cannot sit at the fire, she has to be wiring something, so the camp's
perimeter fills up with her work and nobody asked her to do it.

**Tool: snare cable, pliers, swivels, stakes.** Placed and persistent. The kill happens where she
decided it would happen, before the thing arrived. Rewards reading ground and predicting routes,
punishes panic. This is the identity nothing in the shipped weapon table has, and it is also the
one of the four that is new engine work: nothing in the codebase places a persistent friendly
entity with a lifetime and a trigger. The nearest thing is a motion note, "a field stays where it
was placed" (`js/traits.js:515`) `[code]`, which is projectile behaviour. Combat architecture owns
the substrate; this chapter flags that it does not exist.

**Grows into.** More sets live at once, sets that reset themselves, cable that holds bigger things,
sets that drag what they catch to where she wants it. At high investment she is barely fighting:
she decides in advance where the night happens and the room is a set. What she becomes is the
trapper's version of the Signing, and she is the character who notices.

**Flagship.** What her sets do at the moment they close.

**Fan art hook.** The coil on her hip and the loop already in her hands, a length of wire held in
her teeth, forearms on her knees when she crouches. She is the only one who touches the animals.

**Quotable line.** "I don't chase. I decide where it ends up."

**Negative control.** "Poor thing." She has no sentiment about what she kills, and pity is how a
writer signals that a girl who traps is secretly soft. Her tenderness goes to people only.

### 5.3 VIC, 17. The jacklight.

**Before.** Lives on the county road, works the register at the bait and gas. Her uncle's twelve
volt deer light, her cousin's truck, a battery in a milk crate on an aluminium pack frame.

**Why she was out there.** Shining deer, at night, illegally, the week the ridge turned. She had
been looking at animals' eyes in the dark for two seasons, which is why she was the first person to
see a token on something living and know she was looking at a thing that should not be there.

**Wants.** To not be the one who gets blamed. She wants her name kept out of it, which in a game
about being witnessed is a want the premise structurally cannot grant.

**Wrong about.** She believes she brought this down on the road, that shining taught them where the
people are. She is wrong: it was the survey. Nobody can prove that, including her, and the camp's
suspicion of her is louder than the truth.

**Contradiction.** The person who most wants to be unseen is the only one who can make anything be
seen, and she does it by standing behind the light.

**Fear.** She goes quiet and precise. Fear makes her a professional: she stops swearing, her
sentences get shorter, and she starts giving instructions.

**Tool: a held beam.** The primary attack is not a projectile. She holds light on one thing. Lit
things freeze (which is literally what shining does to a deer) and take damage while lit.

**What the beam does NOT do: it confers sight on nobody.** A lit thing becomes visible to
everything that could already see it, which is the other Signed, the four children, the
thirteen-year-old, and the other three playable. To anyone who has aged out, a lit ridge is still
empty woods, because sight in this world is belief and not illumination: "To an adult the ridge at
night is empty woods," and "Adults cannot see at all... Not cowardice, incapacity"
(`STORY_DESIGN.md:16-17`, `:25-26`). If a 12-volt lamp let the blind adults see, the camp would
light the treeline and nobody would have to be sent, and section 2.3's own line ("the adults keep
it big because they cannot see and light is all they have") would be false. What the camp sees is
the beam itself, and that is the drawback: it is metered by battery, it draws attention, and
holding it pulls things toward her from off screen.

**This is the premise computed rather than labelled.**
`research/narrative/mechanics-as-metaphor/report.md` sec 1 and sec 7 rule that naming a theme is
not mechanics-as-metaphor and that the fix is to change the rule, not the label: here the rule is
that to hurt a thing you must make it seen, and making it seen tells everything else where you are.
The spine's tragedy (being witnessed is the thing they needed, being killed is the thing they were
avoiding, and the player does both in the same act, `STORY_DESIGN.md:51-53`) is inside her trigger
finger. Nobody has to say it, and per that report's sec 7, once it reads, we stop reinforcing it.

**Grows into.** Wider cone, a second lamp on a yoke, an afterimage that keeps counting as being
seen after the thing leaves the beam, and at the far end a lamp that no longer needs the battery,
which is a quietly bad sign about her that the game never explains.

**Flagship.** What the light reveals, which is the one place a build can change what a beam means.

**Fan art hook.** The milk-crate battery on the frame, the beam over her shoulder, and everything
behind her in silhouette. She is the only character who casts light, so every drawing of her is a
lighting exercise.

**Quotable line.** "Hold still. Not you."

**Negative control.** "Let there be light, you ugly bastard." She never performs for anything out
there and she never announces.

### 5.4 HOLLIS, 18. The drip torch.

**Before.** Lake town kid, one season on a burn crew, the youngest on it. She took the job because
it paid and because it was the one place in her life where the rules always worked: you burn into
the wind, you hold the line, you say your escape route out loud. She is the most procedure-bound
person in the camp.

**She is not a pyromaniac.** That was the retired character (`js/data.js:22-23`, title "Pyromania
Badge," desc "Fast, fragile, flammable") `[code]`. Hollis loves fire because fire is predictable,
and the woods have just stopped being.

**Why she was out there.** Laying a test strip on the sand plain ahead of a prescribed burn, which
is also why fire belongs in these woods at all: jack pine barrens are burned on purpose, and
`AUTHENTICITY.md:610-620` already locks post-fire regrowth here as bracken, sweet fern, pin cherry
and blueberry.

**Wants.** To run a burn that goes exactly to plan and get her line signed off, by a boss who
cannot sign anything any more.

**Wrong about.** She believes procedure is protection, and she believes the crew lost people
because somebody broke it. Procedure was never relevant. She is the one who says "we did everything
right," and the game never lets her be right about it.

**Contradiction.** The most cautious person in camp carries the thing that starts fires, and she is
the only one who will burn part of the camp on purpose to hold a line, in a camp whose people are
already burning it by accident.

**Fear.** She narrates. Out loud, in crew voice, calling her own moves as if she still had a crew:
torch is lit, walking the black, watch my line. This is the best actor gift in the cast and it
doubles as the game's diegetic tutorial voice.

**Tool: the drip torch.** Not a jet at faces. A line you walk. Sustained ground fire, denial,
terrain shaping, and the ground remembers where you burned. Mechanically this inherits the shipped
burn plumbing rather than inventing it (`js/data.js:52`) `[code]`.

**Grows into.** Longer black, then **fire that runs upwind at head-fire speed**, which is the thing
that cannot happen and which she says out loud is wrong, then a burn that does not go out at dawn,
and at the top she stops pouring: the ground catches where she puts her foot. She becomes the one
thing she was trained never to be. (Fire that merely creeps into the wind is ordinary backing fire
and the reason you burn into the wind in the first place, so there would be nothing for her to
object to. RECALLED fire-behaviour fact, not from the library.)

**Flagship.** What the black does after she leaves it.

**Fan art hook.** The loop of flame hanging at her knee, the black ground behind her, hard hat
pushed back, **sleeves down, cuffs buttoned, one glove tucked in the belt.** Sleeves down is a hard
PPE rule on a burn crew (RECALLED), so the most procedure-bound person in camp is not drawn on day
one breaking the one rule everybody would see. Save the rolled sleeve for late in her arc, where
slippage is earned.

**Quotable line.** "Say your escape route back to me. Say it."

**Negative control.** "I've always loved fire." That is Junie, and Junie is dead.

**BROCK, 41, and read as sixty. The caretaker.** See section 7.

**PERRY, 40s. The kitchen.** Came up from the road. Ran a fry line at a supper club on the lake for

**The sawyer, 22.** Came off the ridge with the others. He knows trees, ground, water and weather

safety rather than extraction. He co-authors the field guide with Dez, and he says accurate,

**BIRDY, 13. The truck cab. (First to know.)** Too old to see the way the children see, too small

is counting the months, out loud, to anybody who will listen, and that is the game's aging-out
clock spoken by somebody on the other side of it. **She is why the 10 to 15 band exists** (section
9), and she is the twentieth person in the count.

**The mother from the road, 30s.** The camp's political bloc of one. She cannot see, her two
children are here, and she wants the teenagers to go out every night and cannot say that out loud.
She is not a villain and she is not wrong. She is the answer to taxonomy sec 5.1: her interest is
specific, unimpeachable, and it makes her ruthless about somebody else's kid.

- **Contradiction (the richest player-facing content):** Roz. All the formal authority, and she is

- **Formal chart simply wrong:** the camp's own signage. Job titles on hooks with nobody in them, a

## 7. Brock

**Recommendation: he becomes the camp's caretaker, the last employee of a dead institution.** He
holds no rank at all. The rank vocabulary is retired **by the creator's ruling**, which is the
authority; `AUTHENTICITY.md:1320` is the supporting reason ("Baden-Powell 1908 priority is a good
argument, not a clearance. Invent our ranks"). That distinction matters, because the passage
`AUTHENTICITY.md:1320` argues against sits inside STORY_DESIGN section 0, at
`STORY_DESIGN.md:82-83`, and `STORY_DESIGN.md:12-13` declares that section supreme: "This is
settled... Nothing below it may contradict it." One document does not overrule the spine on the
spine's own terms; a creator ruling does. The same move applies to the tone order at
`STORY_DESIGN.md:93`, amended in section 9 by ruling and not by document precedence.

**How he came to have the job, because the arithmetic needs one sentence.** He was the twelfth kid
and has been Signed since he was eleven (`STORY_DESIGN.md:776-777`), thirty years ago, so he is
forty-one. Nobody hires an eleven-year-old caretaker. The camp closed after Troop 12, the boy who
walked down never left, **the caretaker before him kept him on unpaid and taught him the tent
line**, and the payroll caught up years later. Then he stopped being paid again and was just there.
He mows the council ring. He has kept the water working for thirty years. With that sentence,
"thirty years on this ridge" is true and "the last employee of a dead institution" is true, and the
tally post's own reveal line about the man before him covering the post
(`js/feats.js:528`) `[code]` becomes literal.

**Keep the name.** "Brock" is an ordinary man's name, it carries 72 rendered voice clips
(`tools/voice/lines.json`, 72 rows for brock: arc 26, died 23, idle 9, totem 6, blink 3, dawn 3,
intro 2; 72 files in `assets/voice/en/brock`) `[code]`, and it is in every document. The badger
etymology in the sprite comment (`js/sprites.js:3241-3243`) becomes a private joke in a comment.
Everyone at camp calls him Brock with no honorific, because nobody knows what else to call a man
who was here first. In the UI that is a string change, size S, and it has precedent:
`js/feats.js:11-17` records that "feat" became "merit badge" player-side while every identifier and
save key stayed put, and `js/camp.js:852-859` did the same for the spot labels.

**The species goes, and every badger idiom goes with it.** The portrait is explicitly a badger
("Grizzled badger scoutmaster... stripes going grey at the edges, a notched ear, a brow scar,"
`js/portraits.js:425-431`), the world sprite paints the stripes and ears
(`js/sprites.js:3266-3273`), and exactly one of his own lines treats him as an animal: "I can count
dawn-watchers on one paw" (`js/dialogue.js:129`) `[code]`. The other fur references in that file
are critter anatomy and are premise-proof (`js/dialogue.js:103`, `:197`, `:488`). The cost is not
the lines, it is that rewording a line silently orphans its voice clip, because the manifest is
keyed on the exact spoken text and the failure only prints to console (`js/voice.js:77-87`,

**Forty-one, and the gap is the horror.** He says "Thirty years ago I brought twelve kids up this

**His address terms.** He addresses whoever is on duty by a nickname (`js/dialogue.js:101`,

Three of those five bands are the spine's (`STORY_DESIGN.md:15-30`); the other two are named here

# CHAPTER TWO: WHY POWER EXISTS

## The ruling, first

One rule governs items, consumables and the growing tool, and it is the Signing pointed the other
way:

> **Nothing out here works because of what it is. It works because somebody is certain about it.
> The Signed are strong because the woods believe in them. Your kit is strong because you believe
> in your kit, and you are the last age group left that can believe in anything hard enough for it
> to matter.**

The makers out past the treeline fix belief into an animal by hand, with a die and a punch
(`js/finds.js:105-111`, `js/finds.js:141-147`) `[code]`. The player has no die. The player fixes
belief the slow way: carrying a thing, using it ten thousand times, never once doubting it.
**Progression is the player running an unlicensed Signing operation on their own equipment.** They
do not know that is what it is, and nobody tells them.

The run currency stops being an award and becomes salvage: **TIN**, the tokens themselves, off what
you killed. The camp keeps its old ceremony with the wrong object in it. The cost of power is not a
bar and not a corruption stat: **the cost is sight.** Certainty spent on gear is certainty not spent
on the woods, so the woods stop showing themselves at the rate you get strong. That is the spine's
clock (aging out, `STORY_DESIGN.md:28-30`) pulled forward by the build.

## 1. One rule, three cases

Canon gives three facts and I add no fourth: belief is sight and it runs out
(`STORY_DESIGN.md:15-26`); a thing that has truly seen believes forever after and it leaves a mark,
a tin stamp pressed into the fur (`js/dialogue.js:103`) `[code]`; at full belief a cryptid cannot be
hurt, which is why this is the first generation that can bleed (`STORY_DESIGN.md:46-49`). Belief is
not a mood in this world, it is load. Whatever carries enough of it can act. What the canon never
says is who else can pour it, and the answer is the only people left who still have any.

**A carried item makes you stronger because carrying it is a standing statement that it works.** The
ledger is not an inventory, it is a list of things you have decided about. That is why ledger items
are single-acquisition and never leave the build (`BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:406`) `[spec]` and why a
met prerequisite is met forever (`BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:569-570`) `[spec]`: **you cannot un-decide a
ledger item.**

**The keystone is the one place you can un-decide, and it needs its own fiction, not a blanket
rule.** `BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:399-401` `[spec]` says taking a second keystone "offers a straight
trade, and that is the only replacement prompt in the whole design." So a conviction can be
displaced by another conviction. The fiction: the kit takes one mark at a time, and when a second
one sets, the first one lifts off, and the world notices which mark you are wearing. If the
build-architecture owner would rather drop the trade prompt than pay for that, this document does
not object; what it will not do is claim the architecture forbids un-deciding when it plainly
allows it in exactly one place.

**A consumable works because it is the class of object nobody has ever doubted.** A struck match, a
hot drink, a bang. No history required, so no history is needed: the certainty is total, shallow,
and spent in one go. Standing belief compounds, spent belief does not.

**But there is no place to put a consumable, and the earlier draft invented a word for one.** "Tier
active" does not exist anywhere in the eleven design docs or in `js/`, and both halves of the term
are already ruled out: rarity tiers were dropped, not deferred (`BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:1060-1062`,
`:1246`) `[spec]`, and the active skill is capped at exactly one for the whole run, owning one
button (`BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:33`, `:396`) `[spec]`. A class of one-shot objects needs a clock and
a place on the screen, which the architecture calls a slot (`BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:30-31`)
`[spec]`, and there is one and it is taken. **So the consumable half of the belief rule is a
request, not a fact.** Two ways to pay for it, and the choice is open call 27:

- **Cheap:** the four orphan weapons become ledger items riding the existing weapon clock, which
  `BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:30-31` already permits and which costs nothing new.
- **Honest but expensive:** ask the build-architecture owner for a new capped slot against
  `BUILD_ARCHITECTURE.md:33`, with the cost named (a button, a HUD position, a cooldown model, and
  a balance pass across four characters).

**The tool grows forever because belief is a habit, not a decision.** Every swing is one more
statement about what this thing is. The count only goes up, so the tool only goes up, and nothing
needs retrofitting to justify an unbounded ladder where a four-row table sits today
(`js/game.js:574-586`, thirteen lines, and note it is not purely additive: `:581` multiplies
`cdMul` while `:582` adds the rest) `[code]`. It also closes the weapon-pickup question with
fiction instead of a rule: a tool somebody else carried has no history with you, so in your hands
it is an object. **You cannot pick up someone else's certainty.**

The same rule explains the Signed's costume grammar we already ship. They wear our gear for its
SHAPE and not its FUNCTION (`AUTHENTICITY.md:940`), and the inference this chapter draws from that
is that it does nothing for them `[inference]`, which canon does not state. Comprehension is the
power stat. Same objects, two owners, one of them works. It also quietly answers who the one
correctly dressed Signed thing is (`AUTHENTICITY.md:995-1018`): the one who was taught before he
was marked, whose kit works.

The rule gets **no in-world name.** A resource called Belief that behaves like mana is the named
failure (`research/narrative/mechanics-as-metaphor/report.md` sec 1 and sec 11, theme bolted on).
Compute it, never label it. The world names only what people handle.

## 2. The currency is TIN, and the ritual is the Reading

**Recommendation: the survivors keep the camp's ceremony and change what goes into it. The currency
is tin taken off the dead.**

Not adopted badges, because nobody can award anything: there is no institution, nobody is staff and
nobody appointed anyone (`STORY_DESIGN.md:83`). Not a clean replacement either. The ceremony is
thirty years old and unbroken and it is the only rule anybody can remember, which is exactly what
the camp already does with the old job titles (`STORY_DESIGN.md:83-86`). What is different is that
the camp no longer makes the thing it pins up. It comes off a body.

What this buys, stated without overclaiming:

- **The trophy is the same material as the threat**, which `AUTHENTICITY.md:1310` pre-authorised
  when it replaced the sash with a bandolier of tin tokens. That bandolier is now the display
  surface, and the act 1 find becomes a bandolier of eleven with one torn loop.
- **The reason to walk into the woods needs no motivation scene.** The camp runs on tin and there is
  no tin at camp.
- **The board stays complicity, not power.** It used to certify that you learned something. Now it
  certifies what you took.
- **Proof of witness, relocated and not lost.** The token itself is the maker's signature, not
  yours: it records the critter having seen a cryptid, it existed before you arrived, and it can be
  cut off a body you never looked at (`js/dialogue.js:103`, `:197`, `:231`) `[code]`. So the token
  alone does not carry "and it saw you," and this document does not claim it does. **What carries
  your witness is the entry, not the object:** you hand the token in, your name and the date go
  beside it in Dez's hand, and the count is read out. The archive is the signed statement that you
  saw something, which is the job `STORY_DESIGN.md:32-37` gave the badge, and the horror is that
  you are the one who asks for the entry to be made.

**The name passes the naming report's tests, and it is already in our own script.** Brock says "a
little tin stamp" in his first breath (`js/dialogue.js:103`) and "He turns the tin over twice" is
the shipped feat line (`js/dialogue.js:171`) `[code]`. One syllable, function-forward (it names the
material and the object, so flavour rides on top of a name that already does its job,
`research/narrative/naming-nomenclature/report.md` sec 6), and it survives the three tests that
report sets at `:261-275`: read aloud inside a real sentence ("I have got nine tin, is that
enough"), the shout test, spelled the way it sounds. **"Marks" is ruled out on evidence, not
taste:** mark is a live dash trait (`js/data.js:652`) and the transfer-code fingerprint is called
the mark (`js/dialogue.js:180`) `[code]`, and `naming-nomenclature/report.md:382-384` rules out a
name that collides with a common word.

The ceremony is **the Reading.** One sentence, the same sentence every time, and **the number it
reads is the lifetime count, not the wallet.** This matters: `META.badges` goes down on every
purchase (`js/meta.js:99` does `this.badges -= c`, `:100` banks the spend) `[code]`, so reading it
would deliver a number that drops whenever the player shops, in the one moment built to prove
witness. The number that only goes up already exists: `js/meta.js:19` declares `lifetime` with the
comment "total ever collected (drives camp growth)", `js/meta.js:124` does `this.lifetime += paid`,
and `js/meta.js:591` already renders the exact two-number sentence the ceremony wants,
`n(d.lifetime) + ' badges earned in all, ' + n(d.badges) + ' left to spend'` `[code]`. Write the
Reading off `lifetime`.

**Where the Reading is spoken, because the board cannot host a voice.** The Badge Board, Merit
Badges, the Field Guide and the Tally Post are all full-screen DOM modals
(`CAMP_DESIGN.md:1379-1382` `[spec]`, `index.html:487-573`), every one a hard cut out of the world,
and fixing that is called the largest single piece of work in that audit. A count spoken at the
board either plays over a hard cut with no speaker on screen or waits on that work. **So: Brock
says it at the fire, on the way to the board.** No modal work, a speaker on screen, S. If the
creator wants it at the board, the row is blocked on the `CAMP_DESIGN.md:1379` modal unit and is
not S.

**And it must not slow the buy screen.** The taxonomy acquits the menu vendor precisely because a
fast roguelite's between-run shop is where the player wants a quick buy screen
(`generic-premise-failure-taxonomy/report.md:600-602`, `:701`). A spoken line every single time the
player hands in tin is the mid-momentum noise that row warns about. **Constraint: at most once per
night, always skippable, never blocking the purchase.**

**Cookie Money should not become tin.** Keep two currencies, because under the rule they mean
opposite things. Tin is proof of witness. The mid-run coin is the one thing in these woods with no
belief in it at all, which is why the Quartermaster's chair can set the rate by fiat and joke about
it (`js/dialogue.js:456`) `[code]`. Rename it **SCRIP**: old trading-post credit out of a cash box,
worthless everywhere but here, materially correct for a camp store, claiming nothing from anybody.
The one honest risk is that scrip can be misheard as script; it never appears without the coin icon
and a number (`js/sprites.js:3158`) `[code]`, and CHITS is the fallback.

**Internals do not move, but the effort is not S.** The precedent for the rename is in the code's
own words: the player-facing name changed to merit badge while `FEATS`, `flags.feats` and every
save key kept their identifiers (`js/feats.js:11-17`), and the camp spot ids stayed `featboard` and
`totem` while only the prompts changed (`js/camp.js:852-859`) `[code]`. So `META.badges`,
`DATA.COINS` and the transfer fingerprint are untouched and no code dies. **But the currency change
kills a shipped beat and that is a re-render, so the row is M unconditionally.** Brock's act two ask
is "Different makers stamp those tokens out there... Bring me one, if you can pry it loose. I have
wanted to look at one up close for thirty years" (`js/dialogue.js:231-232`) `[code]`. If tokens are
the nightly currency, he has counted hundreds by act 2 and the ask is dead on arrival. **The fix is
to re-scope it explicitly to far-maker tin:** he wants one from the far side of the ridge, stamped
by the maker nobody at camp has seen, which the shipped line's own first clause already sets up. A
reword silently orphans the clip, because `resolvePath` keys on speaker plus spoken text with only
whitespace normalisation as a fallback and `logMissing` only console.info's it
(`js/voice.js:77-94`) `[code]`. So: M, with a re-render in the same wave.

## 3. The cost side

An eerie world that gives power away free is a tone problem, and the honest price is already sitting
in the spine. **Certainty is one faculty, not two. It is what lets you see, and you are spending it
on your hatchet.**

**The price: as the ledger grows, the woods stop showing you what is true.** Three bands on the
ledger count.

1. **Light.** The woods are alive. Non-hostile animals appear, sign is legible, the firefly is
   around.
2. **Settled.** The things that were not trying to kill you stop appearing. Nothing announces it.
3. **Certain.** The baked ground sign stops reading for you. The ground still tells the truth, so
   SIGN NEVER LIES holds in the letter (`AUTHENTICITY.md:1215-1235`), and you lose the pre-room
   roster read exactly as act 3 arrives.

**Two honest qualifications on that third band, because it is the sharpest risk in the chapter.**
First, the letter of the canon survives and the point of the system does not:
`AUTHENTICITY.md:1220` calls layer 2 "the load bearing one, and the recommendation," its payoff is
that the player who learns to read the ground knows the room's roster before the doors close, it
says bake it and do not spawn it so the sign is there because the animal is there, and `:1233-1235`
closes with "the finds are the lesson and the ground is the exam." Band 3 cancels the exam after
the player has passed the lesson. **That is a bigger canon call than a parenthesis can carry, and
it goes to the owner of AUTHENTICITY section 10.7 as open call 11.** Second, the earlier claim that
this costs no new assets is withdrawn: suppressing baked ground sign per room is a per-room switch
on a system built specifically to be baked, and nobody has confirmed it can be suppressed.
UNVERIFIED until they do.

**And the bands are blocked, not merely sized.** The band input is the 3.0 ledger count. That

## 2. Brock

**Recommendation: Brock is the caretaker of Camp Hearthwood.** Not a leader, not staff of anything
that still exists, and nobody's scoutmaster. He is the man the camp kept after the camp stopped
existing.

Why this is the right answer and not a compromise:

- **It explains thirty years with nobody to lead.** A scoutmaster with no troop is a widower with a
  title. A caretaker is a job you can hold alone, and the writing already treats it as one:
  "Thirty years that post stood under canvas because the man before me covered it, and I never once
  asked what a post needs covering for." (`js/feats.js:528`) The man before him was the caretaker.
  He took the job, and chapter one's one-sentence bridge explains how an eleven-year-old came to
  hold it.
- **It is not a rank, so it clears the rank ruling.** Caretaker is a job at a real camp, not a
  programme title. The authority retiring the rank vocabulary is the creator's ruling;
  `AUTHENTICITY.md:1320` is the supporting reason, and chapter one section 7 explains why that
  distinction is necessary.
- **It gives him the exact power the camp needs him to have, and no other.** To a troop he was
  authority. To strangers he is the only person who knows where anything is, which is informal
  power held purely by residence. That is
  `research/communities-premises/gossip-networks-informal-power/report.md` sec 5's **Alignment**
  row, where a person's title is backed by the same trust or obligation that would have made them
  powerful with or without the office. It is not the third case (formal chart simply wrong), whose
  second clause requires the real authority to have moved somewhere else entirely; at Hearthwood it
  did not move, Brock holds all of it. Alignment is also the stronger argument, because it is the
  library's stated reason a player reads authority as earned rather than titular. The camp's empty
  job titles on hooks are where the third case lives, and chapter one puts it there.
- **It keeps every number.** Thirty years, twelve up and one down, eleven he could not save, the
  coward who never fetched a token, the man who will not say the twelfth name. Not one of those
  needed a troop.

**The Quartermaster is renamed out, and this document owes that out loud.**
`AUTHENTICITY.md:1320` names the four words it rules on, and "quartermaster" is one of them. He is
a live spot label (`js/camp.js:849`), a 186-line portrait block (`js/portraits.js:1330-1515`) and 8
rendered clips `[code]`. **Ruling: Roz takes the stock chair (chapter one section 6), the
Quartermaster as a titled character is retired, and his register is the source for Roz's dryness
rather than being thrown away.** Cost, stated rather than hidden: a 186-line portrait and 8 clips
retire, and the "premise-proof line-book rows" claim in section 4 loses him.

### The line that turns out to be the deepest plant in the game

`firstNight` says "Thirty years ago I brought twelve kids up this ridge. I brought one down."
(`js/dialogue.js:189`) The roll book says eleven names in a scoutmaster's block print and a twelfth
line in a child's handwriting: a kid added himself (`js/finds.js:122-130`). If Brock is the twelfth
kid, then "twelve kids" is eleven on the roll plus himself, and "one down" is himself. **The
sentence is true in its arithmetic and false in its person.** He has spent thirty years telling the

## 5. The name

### What a name has to do here

Five jobs from the naming report's title gate (`research/narrative/naming-nomenclature/report.md`
sec 7 and sec 13), plus one this project adds:

1. **Distinctive.** A title of entirely common dictionary words is hard to own.
2. **Sayable and memorable.** Read it aloud cold inside a sentence a player would say, and know what
   people will shorten it to.
3. **Findable.** Search the exact candidate before commit.
4. **Tonally right.** The title is the first tone signal, and a title that names the core loop or
   the core feeling sets expectations more accurately than an abstract theme word.
5. **Availability-aware.** Trademark and namespace, run as a separate gate from the craft gate.
6. **It must not promise a game we are not making.**

### Can the current name survive a reframe? No.

"Merit Badge" names an institution the cast no longer belongs to, and it is the highest priority open
item in AUTHENTICITY, needing a real legal opinion before art and store page
(`AUTHENTICITY.md:1282`, `:1316`, `:1340-1342`). The reframe route (keep the name, redefine badges in
fiction) is closed by the currency ruling. "Massacre" promises splatter comedy, when gore is a toggle
that by house law cannot be load-bearing (`AUTHENTICITY.md:84-95`) and the tone is now eerie played
straight. Both load-bearing words fail job 6 independently. It was an excellent name for the game
being replaced.

### The recommendation: PAST THE TREELINE

- **It is already the world's own phrase, on three shipped surfaces**: `README.md:5` ("the ones out
  past the treeline"), `index.html:464` ("Troop 13 vs. the ones out past the treeline"), and Brock's
  act two beat, "You are past the treeline proper now. Further than I go."
  (`js/dialogue.js:231`) `[code]`.
- **It names the core loop rather than an abstract theme**, which is the naming report's stated
  preference: you leave a sanctuary, you cross a line, you try to be back by dawn.
- **It survives the tone pivot in both directions.** No comedy, no gore, no institution, no cute. It
  withholds the threat's form, which is the first of the horror report's three withholdings.
- **It names nothing we are throwing away**, and it removes both trademark-exposed words from the
  title, which closes AUTHENTICITY's number one item.
- **It says well and shortens well.** "I got to the Sog in Past the Treeline" is a sentence a person
  says.

Weaknesses, stated rather than hidden: three common words make a weaker trademark position than a
coined name; it is quiet, which is a real risk on a loud storefront; and the "Treeline" shorthand
may collide with something nobody has checked.

**The gate has not been run, and it is a blocking condition, not a formality.** There was no search
or trademark access in the sessions that wrote these chapters, so findability and availability for
every candidate above are judgement about the shape of the search space, not results. Before commit:

Brock's mouth, or on the Burn plaque (`js/rooms.js:1781`) `[code]`, and it lands harder there than
it would on a storefront.

# THE STRONGEST ARGUMENT AGAINST

From the completeness critic, stated as it was made, because it is the best argument in the pile and
it is not answered by anything above.

**The case, in one line: the scout frame is the only thing in this project that gave the roguelite a
reason to exist in fiction, and the reboot deletes it before a replacement has been shown to do the
same work.**

**One, the errand engine.** A roguelite needs a reason to walk into the killing woods twenty times.
`STORY_DESIGN.md:32-33` supplied it for free: earning badges is the ordinary in-fiction reason a kid
walks into the woods and needs no justification. Survivors in a sanctuary have the exact opposite
instinct, and it is the instinct chapter one dramatises best: its strongest beat is that three people
who tried to leave are gone and the camp has stopped arguing. A premise whose best writing is about
not going out has to invent, from nothing, a compulsion to go out nightly.

**Two, the thesis.** `STORY_DESIGN.md:32-36` fuses the progression system to the horror: a badge is
a signed statement that you saw something, so levelling up is being Signed in instalments and the
Badge Board is complicity rather than power. That is a rare ludonarrative fusion, it is canon inside
the section the document calls settled (`STORY_DESIGN.md:12`), and it exists only because the cast
earn badges.

**Three, differentiation and the sunk work.** The locked tone order is cute, then wrong
(`STORY_DESIGN.md:93`), and cute is carried by 18 woodland animals, a marshmallow flamethrower and
an Emergency S'more, not by the cast. This document amends the order. What remains is grim teenagers
in dark woods, which is the most crowded shelf in indie horror, and it discards the collision the
title carries and that the project already treats as an asset worth a legal opinion
(`AUTHENTICITY.md:1316`). Measured cost of the cast swap alone: 111 rendered clips plus their
recipes, Brock's pass and re-render, the line-book rows that carry scout vocabulary,
character-keyed sites across nine js modules, the unlock economics in `js/roster.js:8-20`, and the
newest section of the art canon (the teenage amendment the superseded `ART_STYLE.md` carried,
written about Max, Junie and Gus by name; see `docs/preproduction/RULINGS.md`).
And the replacement raises the accuracy bar at the worst moment: `STORY_DESIGN.md:75` demands the
right knot and the right boat, `AUTHENTICITY.md` has a large body of scoutcraft research behind that
demand, and burn-crew, trapline and jacklighting procedure have none. The two day-one misses the
skeptic caught in this very document (sleeves-down PPE, backing fire versus head fire) are evidence
of that gap. Two of the four identities also carry commercial risk: spotlighting deer is a wildlife
crime, and a 16-year-old girl running a trapline is a reputationally loaded hobby on a store page.
(Both RECALLED, not from the library.)

**Four, and this is the sharpest leg: the reboot's real delta is smaller than it looks.**
`STORY_DESIGN.md:61-70` already ruled the camp a sanctuary of survivors who arrived last week with
nobody as staff, and `STORY_DESIGN.md:19-22` already ruled the player characters teenagers. Chapter
one says so itself ("that distribution is not new lore"). So the L-to-XL spend buys cast identity
and pays for it with the badge thesis. **The option the binary skipped:** the ruling was no scout in
the playable cast and the badge economy reframed or replaced, and *reframed* is available.
Non-scout survivors can inherit the dead institution's ritual, which `STORY_DESIGN.md:82-88` has
already written in the camp's own voice. Outsiders being Signed in instalments by paperwork none of
them ever agreed to is more sinister than scouts earning it, and it keeps the thesis, the errand
engine, the tone order, the bestiary, the twist, the currency and the title. Nobody has drafted that
version, and until somebody does, the reboot is being compared against the old game rather than
against its own best alternative.

**What this document owes the argument, honestly:**

- **The thesis is not abandoned, but it is relocated and that relocation is a bet.** Chapter two
  moves the complicity from the badge to the archive entry: the token is the maker's signature, the
  entry is your statement that you saw something, and the Reading is the ceremony. That keeps the
  words of `STORY_DESIGN.md:32-37` true. Whether an archive of other things' tin accuses the player
  as sharply as a badge awarded for witness is not proven and cannot be proven on paper. The
