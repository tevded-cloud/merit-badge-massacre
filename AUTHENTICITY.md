# 1. THE RULE

## PROP COHERENCE

**A prop is not one object. It is a SET of objects that must agree with each other
AND with the ground they sit on.**

A fly rod brings a fly reel, a vest, tippet, a net and MOVING water. A spinning rod
brings a bobber, worms, a tackle box and STILL water. Put the stream kit on a pond and
everyone who fishes clocks it instantly, and everyone who does not fish gains nothing
from the mistake either.

**CORRECT COSTS EXACTLY THE SAME AS WRONG.** Three graded lean sticks with a gap on one
side is the same number of draw calls as two crossed logs. One of them is a teepee. The
other is not any fire that has ever existed.

## THE ACCEPTANCE TEST

> Could someone who actually does this thing tell what happened here?

If yes, it is a prop. If all they would see is mistakes, it is decoration pretending.

Run it out loud before you draw. Name the person: the angler, the pioneering instructor,
the camp waterfront director, the forester, the person who has restored an old building.
If you cannot name who would check it, you are not making a prop, you are making texture,
and texture is fine but it does not get to claim this rule.

## EVERY SET PIECE IS AN INTERRUPTED MOMENT

A prop that is correct also says **WHO LEFT IT AND WHAT THEY WERE DOING WHEN THEY STOPPED.**

A rod on the bank with the line still out and the fly sitting in the water says somebody
walked away mid cast. That is accuracy doing the horror's work.

**Design every kit with its abandoned state.** Not "an arrangement of objects" but "the
exact instant somebody stopped." Cooking is the best example, because it happens in a
strict order and can be stopped at any point in that order, and the point where it stopped
is legible to anyone who has cooked.

The four cheapest interrupted moments, in order of how little they cost:

| Cost | Move |
|---|---|
| One rotation | An object left in the position it is held in, with nobody holding it. |
| One value change | A thing that has run out, dried out, or gone cold on a schedule a person can read. |
| One absence | A counted thing whose count is wrong. Tags, hooks, pegs, columns on a grid. |
| One fill | Debris that names the tool. Knife curls versus axe chips. |

## THE THREE CONSTRAINTS THAT OUTRANK ACCURACY

1. **THE GORE TOGGLE.** If the toggle can remove it, it cannot be load bearing.
   Nearly everything in this document survives gore being switched off, which is
   the point. An opened first aid kit does the whole job of a blood pool and is more
   specific, because a blood pool says something happened and an opened kit says
   somebody TRIED.
2. **THE 40 PX SQUINT.** A trash critter draws about 30 to 48 px. Only silhouette and
   costume SHAPE survive there. Section 9 marks which details are portrait tier and
   which are world tier. A correct detail that cannot resolve is noise, and noise at
   that scale reads as mud.
3. **FACE READABILITY.** Unchanged from ART_STYLE.md. If accuracy costs the face, accuracy loses.

## THE HOUSE LANGUAGE FOR WRONGNESS IS BONE

Owl pellets are packed bone and fur. That is species accurate, it survives the gore
toggle, and a HEAP of them implies time rather than an event. But see 5.2: **a bog
destroys bone.** The Sog is the one act where pellets cannot exist, and their absence
in the one place that otherwise keeps everything is free dread.

# 2. THE CONFIDENCE KEY

Every entry carries a tag. Sub claims inside an entry carry inline tags where they
differ from the entry. **A confident wrong detail is worse than an admitted gap.**

| Tag | Means | What you may do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **[VERIFIED]** | Standard, consistent across the literature and the practice. I would ship it. | Draw it. Put it on screen. Let a character state it. |
| **[LIKELY]** | Right in shape and direction. Specific numbers, names or regional details may vary. | Draw it. Do NOT let a character state a number as gospel. Do not build a puzzle on it. |
| **[NEEDS A HUMAN]** | Real question, unresolved, listed in section 12. | Do not put it on screen. Ask. |

Three standing rules that come out of the key:

- **Order is safe, years are not.** Every decay and succession sequence in this file is
  reliable in ORDER. The specific timescales are estimates. Draw the order.
- **Numbers go on signs, not in narration.** Where agencies genuinely differ (bear hang
  heights above all), put the figure on an in world posted sign so it reads as THIS
  camp's rule rather than as universal law. That is honest and it is also better writing.
- **Regional lists are regional.** Section 5 is written for the upper Great Lakes
  (see 5.0). A document that treats "temperate North American forest" as one ecosystem
  will put the wrong trees next to each other. This one nearly did, twice.

# 3. SCOUTCRAFT

## 3.1 FIRE LAYS

Six lays. Each is a tool. Which one is built says what the builder wanted.

### THE TEEPEE [VERIFIED]

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | A cone. Fist sized tinder bundle on the ground, kindling leaned around it into a cone whose tips touch at a peak, fatter sticks leaned outside that. A deliberate GAP is left in the cone on the windward side. Three material grades always: tinder (finer than a match), kindling (pencil to thumb), fuel (wrist to forearm). |
| **Why** | The cone forces flame up through the middle so every stick is heated by the ones below it, and as the tips burn through it collapses inward and feeds itself once more. Fastest lay to flame, worst lay to leave alone. |
| **Right if** | (1) There is a gap on one side and it faces INTO the wind. A perfectly closed symmetrical cone is a fire built from a picture. (2) Three visibly different thicknesses, thin inside, fat outside. Uniform dowel sized sticks all round is the giveaway. (3) Tinder is INSIDE the cone at ground level, not tucked under the outside. (4) The wood is dead and dry: bark loose and flaking, ends grey. Green wood has tight bark and bright wet cut ends. |
| **Do NOT claim** | The "charred points in, clean butt ends radiating out like spokes" ash signature. That is the STAR fire's fingerprint, not the teepee's. A teepee's butt ends sit at the base of the cone in the hottest part and they burn. A burned out teepee leaves a conical mound of coal and collapsed fragments. Do not name the gap "the door" in on screen text [LIKELY]. |
| **Full kit** | Tinder bundle (birch bark, fatwood, a bird nest twist of dry grass), three sorted wood piles an arm's reach away and NOT in the ring, a striker (matches in a waterproof tin, ferro rod, lighter), a knife with a heap of feathered curls beside it, and the three fire tools: water bucket, shovel, rake. |
| **Belongs on** | Scraped mineral soil in a stone ring, anywhere in the Understory. |
| **Wrong on** | **Peat.** A surface fire on peat drives heat down and can burn UNDERGROUND for weeks and resurface metres away. A bog camp builds on a raised platform of green logs or imported mineral soil, or does not build one. **A burn scar**, quietly: burned wood is punky and worthless as fuel, so a place that looks like it is made of firewood is not. |
| **Abandoned** | Built, correctly, unlit. Tinder inside untouched. Matchbox open on the log, three matches spilled, striker strip unmarked. Wood still sorted into three, so whoever did it was not rushing until the moment they were. |

### THE LOG CABIN [VERIFIED]

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | Two fuel logs parallel, two across them at right angles, stacked in courses, each course shorter and set inward so it tapers to a pyramid rather than a tower. A teepee of tinder and kindling in the hollow middle. Corners overhang. Walls deliberately not chinked tight. |
| **Why** | It burns from the inside out and holds its shape, so it needs no tending and collapses into a broad even bed of COALS. Flame cooks nothing well; coals do. The taper is structural: a straight sided tower topples sideways and throws burning wood out of the ring. |
| **Right if** | Courses step INWARD as they rise. Corners overhang, which is what locks it. There is a teepee loaded in the middle rather than a solid stack with nothing to light. Gaps between the logs in each course. Afterwards, a broad even square-ish grey bed, versus the teepee's conical pile. |
| **Do NOT claim** | Four surviving charred corner stubs. A cabin that burned down to its coal bed burned its corners too. Keep the BED SHAPE as the test. |
| **Full kit** | Split fuel logs of even length, plus whatever the coals are FOR: a Dutch oven with its lid lifter, a grate on two flat stones, a long handled pan. A log cabin with no cooking gear near it is a lay chosen for no reason. |
| **Belongs on** | The camp fire circle, on a night the camp cooks for everybody. |
| **Wrong as** | A signal fire (no smoke, no tall flame) or a one person overnight (eats far too much wood). |
| **Abandoned** | Burned to the coal bed, which is what it was built to do. A Dutch oven sits ON the coals, lid on, coals still heaped on the lid gone white. The lid lifter is in the dirt rather than on its hook. Whatever is inside finished hours ago. |

### THE LEAN-TO [LIKELY]

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | One fat backlog, or a stake driven at a slant, set as a wall. Tinder in its LEE. Kindling leaned against the log at an angle, all sloping the same way. Everything in the fire leans one direction. |
| **Why** | The wind and rain lay. The backlog blocks wind off the young flame, reflects heat forward so the fuel dries as it burns, and gives rain something to run off. It is the lay you can build one handed with cold hands. |
| **Right if** | Everything leans ONE way against ONE log. Sticks leaning in from both sides is a collapsed teepee. Tinder is on the downwind sheltered side. After burning, the backlog is scorched up one face and has untouched bark on the other [LIKELY: the asymmetry is real, the sharpness of the line varies]. |
| **Full kit** | A backlog thicker than anything else present, a drag scar in the duff behind it, sometimes a timber hitch still around it, a wind the geometry agrees with, and a shelter or a body position on the lee side. |
| **Belongs on** | Exposed ground: the ridge, the open edge of the bog, anywhere in the Burn (a burn scar is treeless and wind rips across it). |
| **Wrong in** | A sheltered hollow, where its existence implies a wind that is not there. |
| **Abandoned** | Burning low in a wind hard enough to show in the grass. Nobody has fed it, the leaned kindling has burned through and slumped. A coat on the ground on the warm side with the arms still shaped as though somebody were in it. |

### THE STAR FIRE [VERIFIED]

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | Three to five long, whole, unsawn logs laid like wheel spokes with their ends meeting at a small fire in the middle. You feed it by pushing each log a few inches further in. You kill it by dragging the logs OUT. |
| **Why** | The fire for people who intend to sit still a long time and do not want to spend it cutting wood. Nothing gets processed. Small, controllable, sips fuel, and it can be switched off in four seconds, which is exactly why it is the fire of a night watch. |
| **Right if** | The logs are WHOLE and long, charred only at the inner tips, clean along their whole length. Uniform charring means it was lying in the fire, not fed into it. **Then look at the GROUND:** hours of pushing logs inward leaves radial drag scuffs, each a shallow groove pointing at the centre [LIKELY, and it is the best image in the fire section]. |
| **Full kit** | A seat sat in for hours (a log worn smooth and barkless on top, a folded blanket, a pack as a backrest), something to do while sitting (a whittling project and a pale scatter of curls, a mug, a sheathed knife), and nothing else. One or two people, not a group. |
| **Belongs on** | A watch post, a lookout, the far end of the dock, a trail junction where somebody sat waiting. |
| **Wrong at** | A cooking site (poor coals) and anywhere the long logs could not have come from. |
| **Abandoned** | Two versions, opposites, both correct. **(1)** Logs pushed all the way IN and burned through: nobody moved for a long time. **(2)** Logs dragged all the way OUT, tips still smoking, drag scuffs fresh and long, the middle down to nothing: somebody killed this fire deliberately and fast, which means they wanted the light gone. Version 2 needs no gore, no body and no text. |

### THE KEYHOLE [VERIFIED]

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | A ring shaped like a keyhole: a round chamber where a fire burns all evening, joined to a narrow rectangular channel. You burn in the round part and RAKE COALS across into the rectangle, then set pots or a grate over the rectangle. Its side stones are sized to be the pot rest. |
| **Why** | It solves the one real problem of camp cooking: a fire big enough to be a fire is too hot to cook on, and one small enough to cook on keeps going out. It is the difference between a camp that eats on schedule and a camp that eats at ten at night. |
| **Right if** | There is a TOOL for moving coals: a small shovel, a rake, or a green stick worn and charred at one end. No tool means nobody was raking, which means the geometry is decoration. Flames and fresh fuel in the round part, a low bed with no flame in the rectangle. Both halves flaming is wrong. Both halves the same size is wrong. The rectangle's stones are flat topped and level with each other, because they are load bearing for a pot. |
| **Full kit** | Coal rake or shovel, a grate or two pot rest stones, bail handled pots, a green stick pot lifter or a folded cloth, the wood staged by the ROUND end, and the three basin wash line within a few paces. |
| **Belongs on** | The camp fire circle at its most competent tier. |
| **Wrong at** | A one night stop. Nobody builds a keyhole for one meal. |
| **Abandoned** | Round chamber burned out and cold. Rectangle holds a bed of coals gone grey with one pot still standing on the stones, boiled dry, the bottom scorched blue black. That pot boiled dry, which takes a long time, and nobody came. |

### THE REFLECTOR WALL AND OVEN [LIKELY]

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | Two objects sharing a name. **(1) WALL:** GREEN logs stacked behind the fire, held between two pairs of heavy driven stakes, leaning slightly back. **(2) OVEN:** a folded sheet of tin making an open fronted wedge facing the fire, with a shelf inside, for baking. |
| **Why** | An open fire throws most of its heat straight up. The wall catches the half that would have gone away from you and returns it. The oven works the same way at small scale: bread bakes on radiated heat with no coals underneath. |
| **CORRECTION, read this** | Earlier drafts claimed the backward tilt aims heat DOWN onto a sleeper. That is backwards physics and it fails the moment anyone checks. Tilt the top of a flat wall away from the fire and its normal points up, so heat reflects UP and over. The real reason for the slight lean back is STABILITY: a wall leaning toward a fire falls into it and chars. Say that. Do not say the other thing. |
| **Right if** | (1) SIDE: the wall is BEHIND the fire, opposite the shelter. Art routinely puts it between the fire and the sleeper, which would be a wall between you and your heat. (2) WOOD: green, bark tight, cut ends bright and wet, scorched only on the fire face. A reflector built of dry split firewood is a fire waiting to happen. (3) STAKES: two pairs, clearly heavier than the wall logs. A wall standing upright with no visible stakes is not holding itself up. |
| **Full kit** | A shelter facing it, a sleeping arrangement between the two, a maul or the back of an axe used to drive the stakes, green logs cut to a matching length with fresh cuts. For the oven: the tin wedge, a shelf, a pan of dough, a cloth, and a fire built tall so it throws flame height rather than coals. |
| **Belongs on** | Cold open ground: the ridge. Best in the bog for a wicked reason, because green wood is the only wood a bog has, so the reflector is the easy part and the FIRE is the hard part. |
| **Wrong in** | The Burn. There is no green wood in a burn scar. |
| **Abandoned** | Wall standing, correctly built, fire dead. A bedroll laid out in the right place, the bag open and thrown back rather than crawled out of neatly. |

### THE FIRE SITE ITSELF [VERIFIED]

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | Everything around the lay. A stone ring on ground scraped to mineral soil for a couple of feet in every direction. Sitting logs. Firewood stacked OFF the ground and away from the ring in three grades. And the three tools that live at every ring in a supervised camp: a full water bucket, a shovel, a rake. |
| **Why** | Every part of it is a fire escaping. Duff and pine needles carry fire sideways and underground, hence the scrape. Wood in the ring dries and catches, hence the distance. The three tools are the three things you need: water to drown, a shovel to smother and dig a line, a rake to pull burning material back in. |
| **Right if** | **The stones.** Soot on the INSIDE faces, heat bleached grey or white tops, clean outsides. Uniformly clean stone and uniformly blackened stone are both wrong. **Stone type:** never river rounded cobbles and never porous wet stone, because trapped water flashes to steam and rock can spall. A ring of smooth water worn cobbles is a ring built by somebody who was never taught, which is a perfect deliberate wrongness for survivors who are not scouts. **Seats** worn smooth and barkless on the top only, bark intact underneath. **A killed fire** is drowned, stirred, drowned again: its remains are a dark grey SOUP, not a dry pale ash bed. Wet ash and dry ash look completely different and the difference says whether the fire was put out or simply left. |
| **Full kit** | Bucket (full, standing where it can be grabbed without stepping over the fire), shovel, rake, three graded wood piles or a lashed rack, a hatchet in a stump or in the axe yard rather than loose, a fireguard chart with names on it, the ring. |
| **Abandoned** | The bucket on its side and empty, the spill dried to a dark ring in the dust. The shovel gone entirely. The rake twenty feet out in the grass pointing away from camp. Somebody took the shovel with them and somebody else dropped the rake while running: two different decisions by two different people, told in two objects. The fire itself is dry pale ash. It was never put out, it just finished. |

## 3.2 KNOTS

**The whole point:** wrong knots produce rope that has to be CUT. So a camp run by people
who do not know knots slowly runs out of rope, and every piece of rope in it gets shorter.
That is a free, cumulative, entirely visual piece of characterisation.

| Knot | Job | Right if [tag] | Wrong version, which is the one to draw deliberately |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bowline** | A fixed loop that will not slip, will not tighten on what is inside it, and does not jam after loading. | The TAIL finishes on the INSIDE of the loop, alongside the loop's own leg. [VERIFIED] | Tail outside the loop is the cowboy bowline. It holds, and it is the version that gets corrected. Tail around the wrong side capsizes into a slip knot under load, which is worth drawing on a rope that failed. |
| **Clove hitch** | Starts and finishes nearly every lashing. Fast, tied mid rope or at an end, grips under a steady perpendicular pull. | An X on ONE face of the pole, two clean parallel wraps, ends emerging from under the X in opposite directions. On a load bearing square lashing the starting hitch goes on the vertical spar just BELOW the horizontal, so weight pushes the joint down into it. [VERIFIED] | Two turns that do not cross are just two turns and fall off when the pole is lifted. An X on both faces is tied wrong. |
| **Taut-line hitch** | The adjustable hitch on a tent GUY LINE. Grips under load, slides by hand when slack. | It sits as a small barrel of wraps ON the standing part, partway along the line where it can slide, not jammed against the stake. The STAKE leans AWAY from the tent at about forty five degrees. [VERIFIED] Standard teaching is two turns inside the loop then one outside, but variants exist and some handbooks now teach the midshipman's hitch. [LIKELY, do not build a puzzle on the count.] | A stake leaning TOWARD the tent is the classic wrong detail and pulls out under any load. A plastic line tensioner on a hairy natural rope in an old camp is an anachronism a camper feels without naming. |
| **Sheet bend** | Joins two ropes of DIFFERENT thickness. | Both TAILS finish on the same side. [VERIFIED] | Tails on opposite sides is the left handed sheet bend, measurably less secure, and the standard thing an instructor points at. |
| **Square (reef) knot** | Joins the two ends of the SAME line around a bundle: a bandage, a bedroll, a package. Nothing else. | The two loops lie FLAT and parallel. [VERIFIED] | Loops crossed at right angles is a GRANNY, which slips and jams. A granny on a bandage is an outstanding one stroke detail: unremarkable to most players, instantly wrong to anyone who was ever taught it. A square knot used to join two ropes of different diameter will roll and spill under load. |
| **Timber hitch** | Drags or hoists a log, and STARTS a diagonal lashing because it is the one knot that closes a gap as it tightens. | Three or more tucks, all spiralling the same way along the standing part. [VERIFIED] Add a half hitch near the leading end and it is a killick, which makes the log follow nose first. | Two tucks slips, one is not a knot. A clove hitch used to drag a log is the common wrong version, and the log will have swung broadside across the drag path instead of lying along it. |
| **Round turn and two half hitches** | Ties off to a post or a ring. | [VERIFIED] | |
| **Trucker's hitch** | Tensions a ridge line or a lashed load. | [VERIFIED] | |
| **Cleat hitch** | Ties a boat to a dock cleat. | [VERIFIED] | A mooring line that is a smooth curve with two bare ends is a sentence with no verb. |
| **Constrictor hitch** | Ties a rolled bundle. Grips a cylinder and will not slip. | [VERIFIED] | A bow or a granny on a bedroll is a legitimate character read, and it should be a decision. |

## 3.3 LASHINGS

**The fastest correctness test in all of pioneering:** are the two spars touching where they cross?

| | Square lashing | Diagonal lashing |
|---|---|---|
| **Use when** | Two spars cross at right angles and ALREADY TOUCH. | Two spars cross and SPRING APART and must be pulled together. It is the brace. |
| **Starts with** | Clove hitch on the vertical, just below the horizontal. | TIMBER HITCH around both, hauled up so it draws them into contact. |
| **Wraps** | Three or four turns around both spars on a consistent square path, laid neatly beside each other, NOT heaped. | Three wraps along one diagonal, three along the other. It literally looks like an X of two rope bundles. |
| **Then** | Two or three FRAPPING turns taken between the spars around the wraps and hauled tight. Clove hitch to finish. | Frapping. Clove hitch. |
| **Reads wrong if** | It looks like an X. Square lashing wraps go around the joint squarely. | It does not look like an X, or it started with a clove hitch. |

[VERIFIED for all of the above.]

**FRAPPING IS THE THING ART ALWAYS DROPS.** Wraps carry the load; frapping turns take the
SLACK out of the wraps. Without them the joint has play, and a structure whose joints have
play racks, sways and walks apart. A visible narrower band of turns crossing BETWEEN the
two spars, at right angles to the wraps, biting into them, is the single detail that tells
a pioneering literate player we knew what we were doing.

| Lashing | What it does | Right if [VERIFIED] |
|---|---|---|
| **Shear** | Joins two spars SIDE BY SIDE so they can be opened into an A. | MANY parallel turns, a long band, noticeably more than the three or four of a square lashing, sitting near one end of two poles that then splay. Wrapped deliberately LOOSE: if you wrap shear legs tight, the legs will not spread. A short tight band on splayed legs could not have opened. |
| **Tripod (figure eight)** | Same job for three spars. | The rope passes over one leg and under the next in alternation rather than banding all three together, with frapping in the two gaps. |
| **Round** | Joins two spars end to end to make one longer spar. | ALWAYS IN PAIRS, one at each end of the overlap. A single one lets the spars pivot. |
| **Floor** | Decks a bridge or platform. | |

**Rope sized to the spars.** Thin cord on fat spars is wrong and so is hawser on
broomsticks. Natural fibre goes fuzzy exactly where it has been worked, so a used lashing
has a hairy halo and the rope either side of it is smooth. [VERIFIED]

**SCALE NOTE:** frapping is not paintable on a 40 px critter. It IS paintable at camp scale
on the dock, the gateway and the wash stand, and those are the three places a knowledgeable
player will look hardest.

## 3.4 PIONEERING [VERIFIED]

**Vocabulary a knowledgeable player will notice us using correctly:** SPAR (a pole), BUTT
and TIP, TRESTLE (the H or A frame that is the basic unit of every big build), LEDGER (the
horizontal the legs lash to), TRANSOM (a crosspiece), RACKING or BRACE (the diagonal),
GUY LINE, ANCHOR or HOLDFAST (pickets driven in a line and lashed nose to tail, commonly
3-2-1: three pickets, then two, then one, roped in series).

Nails split poles, rust, and cannot be recovered. Rope can be untied, recoiled and used for
the next thing, which matters when a camp owns finite rope and infinite trees. Spars are
stored HORIZONTALLY off the ground under cover, because a pole stored leaning takes a
permanent bend and a pole on the ground rots.

**THE BUILD ORDER IS THE DEEPEST CHECK AND IT IS FREE TO US, because our player rebuilds
the camp.** See 4.4.

| Project | What it is | The checks | Abandoned |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lashed dock** | A pier on TRESTLES standing on the lakebed, stringers trestle to trestle, decking lashed across, guyed back to shore anchors. Legs stand on FOOTPLATES so they do not sink into mud. | Waterline: everything below it is slimy dark and green, everything above is dry grey and fuzzy. Wood does not weather uniformly through a waterline. DECK worn pale in a strip down the middle, grey and lichened at the edges. Footplates under the legs. [Honest: many real camp swim docks are FLOATING on barrels or foam. Both are authentic. The lashed one is the one our player can plausibly build.] | A lashed dock thirty years on is a line of spars lying on the bottom in the shape of the trestles that fell, because the rope went first and the wood second. The dock is gone and the DRAWING of the dock is still there under the water. |
| **Monkey bridge** | One FOOT rope, two HAND ropes, carried on shear leg A-frames at each bank, guyed back to ground anchors, hand ropes laced down to the foot rope with stringer loops. | A-frames LEAN AWAY from the span, never vertical, never leaning in. Back guys to picket holdfasts, not just a rope round a tree. Hand ropes HIGHER than and OUTSIDE the foot rope, at hip or waist height. Stringer lacing in repeating loops, which is what stops the hand ropes swinging out of reach the moment weight goes on. | Foot rope across and tensioned. One hand rope across. The other still coiled on the near bank with its end tied to the near A-frame. It is a bridge you cannot cross, one rope short, and the missing rope is the one that would have made it safe. |
| **Trestle tower** | Four faces of lashed frames, a platform of transoms with decking, diagonal bracing on EVERY face, four guys to four ground anchors. Built lying down and WALKED UP with ropes. | Count the diagonals: every rectangular face gets one, and they are DIAGONAL lashings, because bracing spars do not touch where they cross. A rectangle of four poles is a hinge and will fold flat. Four guys to four anchors, tensioned. And a way up, because a tower with no ladder is a sculpture. | Standing, guyed, complete, with binoculars on a nail at the top platform and a notebook underneath weighted with a stone, open, the last entry in a different hand from the ones above it. ## 3.9 THE CAMP DAY [VERIFIED on shape, see 11 on vocabulary]

The real order, with the words that get used: reveille, personal time and bedding AIRED,
morning colours, breakfast, inspection, instruction blocks, lunch, a rest hour in the heat,
free swim and patrol projects, retreat and evening colours before dinner, dinner, cleanup,
the evening programme, a late informal meeting for the seniors, then lights out and quiet
hours.

The rest hour is in the middle because that is when it is hottest and when a tired teenager
gets hurt. The flag goes up before breakfast and down before dinner because those are the
two moments the whole camp is already gathering.

**THE ONE THING WORTH KEEPING FROM THIS ENTIRE ENTRY, because almost nothing else in it is
drawable:** the flag is UP, it is dark, and it has been up for days, so the halyard has
wound itself round the pole in the wind and the flag has stopped moving freely. The one
thing a camp does every single evening without fail is lower the flag.

The other thing worth keeping is the CAMPFIRE PROGRAMME's shape, because it is a pacing
gift: it opens LOUD and funny and gets quieter through the evening, ending with a slow song
and a short talk. That arc is why a camp goes silent at the end of a night rather than being
switched off.

**READ SECTION 11 BEFORE PUTTING ANY OF THESE WORDS IN THE SCRIPT.** Several of them are
closer to a specific organisation's programme vocabulary than the research assumed.

## 4.2 SCALE, AND WHY THE REAL DISTANCES CANNOT BE DRAWN [VERIFIED as a conclusion]

Camp Hearthwood is 1560 by 1080 world units (`CAMP_G`, js/camp.js:14). At any plausible
scale that is ONE TROOP CAMPSITE, not a summer camp, and every 200 foot separation in this
section is longer than the camp is wide.

**Do NOT present a measured figure in feet.** Earlier research converted a 43.66 unit scout
sprite into 1.51 inches per unit and got "196 by 136 feet." The arithmetic is fine and the
premise is not: this is a top down three quarter view where a standing figure's drawn height
conflates height and depth, and every top down game draws characters enormous on purpose.
The conclusion survives without the number.

That is the right call for a game, and it means:

> **PRESERVE THE ORDER AND DIRECTION OF THE SEPARATIONS, NOT THEIR MAGNITUDE.**

A person who knows camps will not measure our pixels. They check the SEQUENCE: is the
latrine furthest from the water and downhill of the tents; is the fire between the tents and
the trail rather than upwind of the tents; is the food store away from where people sleep.
Get the order right and the compression is invisible. Get the order wrong and no amount of
space saves it.

**THREE TOOLS THAT BUY DISTANCE WITHOUT SPACE, and this is the best layout idea in the file:**

1. A path that LEAVES THE BOUNDS. The latrine is off screen at the end of a walk, which is
   both honest and free.
2. A screen of brush that hides one zone from another so they read as further apart.
3. A sign that states a distance the player cannot pace out.

Concretely: do not try to place a latrine inside `CAMP_G`. Put a signed path heading
southwest off the tent row that leaves the walkable rectangles, and let the player never
arrive. Same trick for the water tank and the wood lot. **Three zones for the price of three
paths and three signs.**

# 5. THE THREE ACTS

## 5.0 THE GEOGRAPHY LOCK: THE UPPER GREAT LAKES [VERIFIED, and this is an owner decision]

**Pick northern Michigan, northern Wisconsin or northeast Minnesota as the unstated real
place. Never say it out loud. It should only be inferable from the species mix.**

One glacier explains all three acts. The Laurentide ice sheet left buried ice blocks that
melted into kettle holes with no inlet and no outlet, which is how a true rain fed bog forms.
The same glaciation left sand plains that grow jack pine, and jack pine burns on a short
cycle. The ridges between are northern hardwood. **It is the only region in North America
where our three acts are neighbours rather than three different vacations.**

**What it buys, tested against our own roster:** elk, American badger, beaver, great blue
heron, snowshoe hare and feral hogs all become simultaneously true. On an eastern hardwood
ridge alone the badger is flatly wrong and true bogs are rare.

**What it fixes specifically:** `quarrybadger` (js/data.js:250). The American badger is a
grassland, prairie and sandy barrens digger of the plains and upper Midwest. In a Wisconsin
jack pine barren on sandy soil it is exactly right, and the burn opens precisely the ground
it hunts. Wisconsin is the safe anchor. [LIKELY on northern Michigan density specifically.]

**Two things this changes downstream and both matter:**

1. **Overbrowse looks different here.** The hay scented fern monoculture is an Allegheny and
   Pennsylvania signature, not a Great Lakes one. Here the classic, textbook, forester
   recognised signature is **eastern hemlock and northern white cedar that cannot regenerate**:
   big old hemlocks with nothing under them, and a hard browse line on cedar in the winter deer
   yards. That is a stronger image for us anyway, because "the old trees are here and there are
   no young ones anywhere" is legible horror without a word.
2. **Post fire regrowth looks different here.** Fireweed carpets are a western, Alaskan and
   boreal image. In upper Great Lakes jack pine barrens the first flush is **bracken fern,
   sweet fern, pin cherry and above all BLUEBERRY**, which resprouts from surviving rhizomes and
   is the single most famous thing about those burns. People drive to burned over jack pine
   country to pick blueberries. [LIKELY.]

**Free props the lock supplies:** glacial erratics (isolated boulders sitting on ground that
did not make them), and four foot logging era stumps sawn flat from the 1890s cut.

**Feral hogs stay, but they are NOT NATIVE here and are under active eradication.** Label them
in world as escaped stock, not as forest wildlife. That is thematically rich (an animal that
should not be here) and somebody who knows will notice if we do not.

Reindeer lichen in pale grey white mats on the driest hummock tops, which crunches loudly when dry and goes silent when wet. | **Every step squeezes water out of the moss, so a footprint fills with brown water and stays for a long time.** A line of water filled prints leading away across the mat, and no walker. |
| **ZONATION** [VERIFIED] | Outside in: upland forest edge; a LAGG, a wet moat where runoff collects, often the wettest part, with alder and winterberry; a belt of black spruce and tamarack; a knee high shrub mat of leatherleaf, Labrador tea, bog laurel and cranberry; open sphagnum with cotton grass and the carnivores; a black open pool in the middle. The carnivorous plants are in the middle precisely because that is where the nitrogen shortage is worst. | Pitcher plant: a squat red veined vase half full of rainwater and drowned insects, sitting LOW in the moss, with a strange nodding maroon flower on a tall separate stalk. Round leaved sundew: tiny, a rosette of red paddles covered in glistening sticky droplets that look exactly like dew and NEVER DRY, even at noon. Anyone who has been in a bog has crouched to look at these. | **A sundew is a thing that looks like water and is a trap, at the scale of an insect, growing in thousands.** That is our entire fiction rendered as a real plant and we did not have to invent it. |
| **THE WATER** [VERIFIED] | Brown black like strong tea, and it is CLEAR, not cloudy. In sun the shallows go amber gold. In shade it is nearly black and acts as a perfect mirror. Peat and conifer needles leach tannins that absorb blue light and pass red. Because light cannot penetrate, nothing roots in the deep water and the bottom is bare peat. | **Clear but dark is the tell.** Muddy water comes from suspended sediment and is a different thing. The mirror means you cannot see into it, and the reason is CHEMISTRY, not fog, which is a much better reason. Acidic bog ponds are often fishless or hold only yellow perch, mudminnow and brook stickleback. **No bass, no trout.** Anyone who fishes clocks a bass in a bog instantly. | See below. |
| **WHAT A BOG KEEPS, AND WHAT IT DESTROYS** [VERIFIED, and this is the most useful correction in the document] | Cold acid anoxic peat preserves SKIN, HAIR, NAILS, LEATHER and WOOL, which is why bog bodies come out looking like themselves. **IT DISSOLVES BONE,** because the acid strips the calcium phosphate. The famous bog bodies have faces and no skeletons. | **THEREFORE: OWL PELLETS, WHICH ARE PACKED BONE, DO NOT SURVIVE ON THE SOG MAT.** Their absence in the one act whose ground otherwise keeps everything is real, checkable, free dread. **The camp shorthand: the Understory keeps HARDWARE, the Sog keeps SKIN and leaves NO BONES, the Burn keeps only METAL.** Also: preservation applies to what is IN the peat, not what is lying on top of it. A boot on the surface for thirty years is a weathered curl of leather. A boot pulled OUT of the peat is a boot. Which one the game hands the player is a choice to make on purpose. | The Sog hands the player an intact 1996 boot, laced, double knotted, lying where thirty years should have taken it. |
| **THE TREES** [VERIFIED] | Two kinds and they are opposites. **The stunted ancient:** a black spruce on the open mat, three metres tall, five centimetres thick, a tuft at the top, a bare middle, a skirt where the lowest branches have layered into the moss and rooted. **The drowned:** a stand of grey barkless spars standing dead in still water, killed when a beaver dam raised the water over their roots. | **The inversion is the payoff and it rewards exactly the person who notices: the scrubby little trees in the bog are the OLD ones, easily a hundred and fifty to two hundred years, and the big healthy trees up on the ridge are the young ones.** Tamarack is a DECIDUOUS conifer: gold in October, then bare all winter looking dead, and it is not. Hang BEARD LICHEN on the spruce, which is a cheap silhouette modifier on the existing conifer painters and instantly separates an act 2 spruce from an act 1 hemlock. Beard lichen means old, damp and undisturbed. Never in act 3. | **THE HERONRY.** Great blue herons nest colonially in the tops of drowned timber: dozens of huge stick nests in dead trees standing in water, everything below whitewashed solid, dropped fish and bones underneath. An animal built this by flooding a forest, and another animal built a city in the tops of it. Nothing about it is supernatural and all of it looks wrong. Also perfect Lanes geometry: thin vertical solids, long sightlines, no cover from ranged. |
| **THE BEAVER'S WORKS** [VERIFIED] | Six objects, one kit: the DAM (a woven arc of sticks and mud with water spilling over the crest); the LODGE (sticks and mud, underwater entrance); CUT STUMPS pointed like a sharpened pencil with paired incisor gouges and pale chips at the base; DRAG TRAILS worn from water to cutting; CANALS, straight water filled ditches dug into the woods to FLOAT wood out, because dragging a log is slow; and SCENT MOUNDS of mud at territory edges. | Freshness reads off the chips: pale unweathered chips and a bright cut face is days; grey chips and a dark stump is last year. A lodge steaming from its top vent in cold weather is occupied. In autumn there is a food cache, a raft of cut branches jammed into the mud beside the lodge. **RECOMMEND MAKING THIS THE FICTION: the beaver dam is WHY the bog is there.** The flowage drowned the stand, the water went stagnant and acid, sphagnum took it, and the bog is the aftermath of an animal's engineering. That justifies our beaver weight of 4 perfectly. | **A dam is repaired at night, in hours, in response to the SOUND of running water.** Break a dam and it is fixed by morning. A dam with a fresh breach that has NOT been repaired means the beavers are gone, and a trapper reads that instantly. |
| **NIGHT** [VERIFIED] | A spring bog chorus is deafening. Spring peepers give a single bird like peep, thousands at once, physically painful up close. Wood frogs quack like ducks. Green frog gives a loose banjo string plunk. Over it, a sandhill crane's bugling rattle carries more than a mile and sounds prehistoric. Amphibians dominate because acid fishless water has no predators for tadpoles. Each species calls on a different frequency so a chorus is layered rather than mush. | **A chorus goes silent when you approach and starts again behind you, in a moving hole of quiet that follows you across the mat.** Real, free stealth mechanic. Note: BULLFROGS want warmer more fertile water, so a true bog is peepers, wood frogs and green frogs, not the deep jug-o-rum. Blackflies in May and June crawl into the hairline and bite exactly where a hat band sits, which anyone from the north woods recognises instantly. | **Wood frogs freeze solid in winter, with ice in the body cavity and the heart stopped, and thaw alive in spring.** A literal thing that comes back, and it is true. |
| **Methane and will-o'-the-wisp** | Methane genuinely bubbles up through peat and releases when you step. [VERIFIED] Marsh gas IGNITING is **FOLKLORE with weak physical support** and must not be presented as science. [NEEDS A HUMAN if it ever becomes load bearing.] | Our fiction is that cryptids are real only while believed in, so bog folklore treated AS folklore is exactly on theme and we lose nothing by being honest about it. | |

## 5.3 ACT 3: THE BURN

### PICK A YEAR AND COMMIT. RECOMMENDATION: TWO TO THREE YEARS AFTER A STAND REPLACING CROWN FIRE.

That gives trunks black low and greying high, the first flush of colour on the ground, and no
loose ash, because ash is fine and alkaline and blows and washes away within weeks. **A burn
goes from BLACK to SILVER GREY over roughly three to eight years** [LIKELY], so if we want
black, the fire must be recent, and if it is recent we do not get tall regrowth. Pure loose ash
is a weeks old detail, not a years old one.

| Feature | What / why | Right if | Abandoned |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Which fire happened** [VERIFIED] | **SURFACE:** burns litter and understory, flames under two metres, blackens lower trunks to a clear char height, scorches the lower crown brown, big trees live with green tops. **CROWN:** climbs into the canopy and kills everything, burning off every needle and fine branch. **GROUND:** smoulders in deep duff or peat for weeks, kills trees by cooking their roots, leaves intact looking trees that are dead and deep pits where stumps burned out underground. | **Read the char height.** A clean horizontal upper limit to the black on every trunk with live crowns above is a surface fire and the stand is fine. Black all the way to the top with no twigs left is a crown fire and the stand is dead. Thick barked mature pine survives surface fire routinely; that is what it evolved bark for. | |
| **SEVERITY IS ALWAYS PATCHY, and this is the most useful fact in the section for level design** [VERIFIED] | A real burn is a MOSAIC: unburned islands survive in wet draws, on north facing slopes and in the lee of rock outcrops; edges burn lightly; cores burn severely. | **A uniformly burned map reads fake to anyone who has walked a fire scar.** | **An unburned green island in the middle of a black one is the only shade, the only cover and the only living thing for hundreds of metres. Every animal in the burn is in it. So is everything else.** One green island per act 3 room, placed as the contested ground, is accurate AND good combat design, and it gives the act visual relief so the orange does not become wallpaper. |
| **Standing dead** [VERIFIED] | A crown fire snag is a BLACK POLE with a few stubs and **NO FINE BRANCHES AT ALL**, because twigs burn first. Thick barked pines carry deeply cracked cubical char. Thin barked trees shed bark in sheets within a year or two. At the base, a CAT FACE: a triangular scar burned into the wood on one side, deepened by every subsequent fire, so a big one is a tree that has survived several. | **Games always draw burned trees with full branch structure, which is the single most common burn mistake.** The char is DIRECTIONAL: it runs higher on the side the fire approached from, which is a permanent record of which way the fire ran, and every wildland firefighter reads it. [Describe the cubical char, do NOT name it "alligator char" in text; LIKELY on the term.] | Standing dead trees are called hazard trees, and the standing rule is that you never camp or sleep under one. A tent pitched under a snag says whoever pitched it did not know or did not care. |
| **How they come down** [VERIFIED on mechanism, LIKELY on years] | They go over whole with the root plate (roots burned or rotted) or SNAP at the cat face, which is the weak point. They fall in the prevailing wind direction, so an old burn has trunks jackstrawed across each other with a strong directional grain. | Small thin barked trees like aspen and birch fall within three to ten years; big pines can stand twenty to forty. Do not print those years. What is certain is the SEQUENCE: a forest of spars, then spars over a wall of young growth, then scattered spars, then nothing standing. | **The burn in wind is a chorus of creaks, knocks and unattributable loud cracks, and a crack behind you might be the woods or might not be, and there is no way to tell.** Free tension for one audio loop. |
| **The ground** [VERIFIED] | Fine white or grey ash where fuel burned completely; black char where it did not. Under both, bare mineral soil, often reddened or orange where heat oxidised the iron. Punched through it: ROOT AND STUMP HOLES in the shape of a burned out root system, sometimes deep, hidden under ash. | **HYDROPHOBIC SOIL:** intense fire vaporises organic compounds that recondense as a waxy layer a few centimetres down, and the soil then repels water. Rain sheets off instead of soaking in, which is why burn scars produce flash floods out of all proportion to the rainfall, for years. Sooted rock spalls when heated, so dark rock carries fresh pale flake scars, and soot washes off in vertical stripes below runoff lines. | **Stepping into a hidden stump hole is a common real injury on a burn. A hole in the ground shaped exactly like the roots of a tree that is no longer there is genuinely unnerving and completely ordinary.** |
| **Succession** [VERIFIED on order, CORRECTED on species per 5.0] | **WEEKS:** black and grey, ash, no green, and the smell of wet charcoal after every rain, which lasts for months. **YEAR 1:** the first green comes from UNDERGROUND, not from seed. Bracken fern fiddleheads push straight up through black ash. Aspen root suckers come up in thousands from an intact root system, all the same age, all pencil thin. **BLUEBERRY resprouts from surviving rhizomes.** Sweet fern, pin cherry and raspberry germinate from seed banked in the soil for decades waiting for exactly this heat and light. **YEARS 3 TO 10:** shrub and sapling stage. **YEARS 10 TO 30:** the aspen and jack pine wall closes over your head, it is dark again, and the visible black disappears. | **DO NOT use fireweed as the act's colour.** That is a western and boreal image; see 5.0. **THE COUNTERINTUITIVE TRUTH WORTH BUILDING THE ACT ON: a burn is not dead, it is the MOST alive place in the forest.** Years three to ten is the richest wildlife stage there is: deer and elk for the new browse, bear for the berries, hare for the dense stems, cavity birds for the snags. A burn drawn as a lifeless wasteland is the mistake, and it is what justifies doe at weight 12 and elk. | A blueberry picker's bucket, tipped, in the ash. Same interrupted moment as the morel picker and it is the correct regional version. |
| **Serotiny** [VERIFIED] | Jack pine and lodgepole cones are sealed shut with resin and only open at around fifty degrees Celsius, which in practice means a fire. The seed sits stored on the tree for years, is released by the fire that kills the parent, and lands on the ash bed, which is exactly the bare mineral seedbed the seedling needs. The species cannot persist without fire. It has bet its entire reproduction on burning, and it wins because it is the only thing with seed on the ground the week after. | A closed serotinous cone is small, hard, curved and visibly clamped shut, unlike an open pine cone. **Match the cone to the tree it fell from, which is our rule in miniature:** jack pine cones small, hard, curved, closed; white pine long, curved, sticky with resin; spruce small and papery. | A cone that only opens in fire, lying open and empty in the ash. |
| **Post fire fungi** [VERIFIED] | Black morels fruit in enormous numbers in the FIRST SPRING after a conifer fire, and commercial pickers follow burn scars. Alongside: Pyronema, a pinkish orange crust on burned soil within weeks, and small charcoal loving cup fungi. These are pyrophilous species adapted to colonise sterilised, alkaline, competition free ground; they get one season with no competitors. | **A morel is a hollow pitted cone on a hollow stem and it looks like nothing else. Morels in numbers means the fire was last year: a mushroom that tells you the date.** And the negative: fly agaric is MYCORRHIZAL and needs a LIVING partner tree, so it physically cannot fruit in a stand replacing burn where its partners are dead. | |
| **Water** [VERIFIED] | Rain runs off instead of soaking in, so streams flash hard. The first big rain runs the water black grey with ash, then orange brown with sediment. Banks slump because the roots holding them are dead, the channel widens and braids, and fallen snags choke it into new pools. | Ash raises pH and strips oxygen, so the first big post fire rain causes fish kills and a burned watershed's stream can be dead for a season. **Long term it gets BETTER than before,** because woody debris makes pools and cover. So the burn stream is bad now and good later, which is the same arc as the whole act. Our theme 3 water is a flat grey blue; post fire water is OPAQUE, grey then orange, and that gives us a third genuinely distinct water in a game whose three water colours currently all read as "water." | A crossing that was fordable and is now a four metre braid of grey water over a bed of black sticks, with the old trail arriving at it and stopping. It also gives the Funnel archetype a real reason: **the crossings moved, so there is one place to get across, and it is the obvious place, and everything knows it.** |
| **Sound and light** [VERIFIED on the physics, ART DIRECTION CALL on the consequence] | No canopy means nothing to break wind into the soft rush people associate with forest. You get bare wind noise plus creaks, knocks and groans as dead trunks flex. Sound travels far because there is no foliage to absorb it and hard ground reflects. Light is full, harsh, unfiltered and shadowless except for thin hard stripes cast by the spars. Leaves are the forest's acoustic absorber AND its light diffuser, and a burn has removed both at once. | **Anyone who has stood in a burn says the same two things: it is loud in wind, and it is glaring. No shade anywhere is what people mention first.** At night, a full moon and the entire starfield, because there is nothing between you and the sky. Best sound in the act: **in summer the loudest thing in a burn can be the beetle larvae inside the trunks, a faint continuous chewing and ticking, audible if you put your ear against the wood.** | **Everything you touch marks you.** Boots, trousers and hands are black within minutes and it does not brush off. |

**FIRE BEETLES** [LIKELY]: Melanophila beetles detect infrared and smoke and fly TO a fire
while it is still burning, to lay eggs in freshly killed wood. Reported detection distances
vary widely and some large claims are disputed. Say "from a long way off," never a number. It
is still the most alien true fact available to act 3: insects that fly toward fire.

**KIRTLAND'S WARBLER** [LIKELY, and nobody flagged it]: nests only in young jack pine, five to
twenty years after fire, in almost exactly this country. A bird whose whole species depends on
things burning. **Do not make it an enemy, it is endangered and that would read badly. Make it
a field guide plate.**

## 5.4 TRAIL MARKS

| Mark | What / why | Right if | Wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Painted blaze** [VERIFIED on form, LIKELY on conventions] | A painted rectangle roughly two inches by six at eye height on a trunk, on the side facing the walker, and painted on BOTH sides of the tree so the trail works in both directions. Spaced so from one you can usually see the next, closer where the tread is unclear. It answers one question: am I still on it. | A DOUBLE BLAZE, two rectangles one above the other, means a turn is coming. [LIKELY: in many systems the top blaze is offset toward the side the trail turns, but this is common and NOT universal, and some clubs use a plain double blaze for caution only.] Colour codes are system specific and not national; do not lean on colour. | A painted blaze in act 3 would have burned off, which is useful: **the blazes stop where the fire started.** |
| **Cut blaze** [VERIFIED] | The old way, before paint. Chop a patch of bark off with a hatchet, exposing pale sapwood. The tree cannot heal, only seal, so callus grows in from the edges and an old cut blaze becomes a raised, dark, lens shaped scar with a lip. | Freshness is completely readable: bright pale wood and clean tool marks is DAYS; grey wood with the edges swelling is a FEW YEARS; a dark lens with a heavy lip is DECADES. **A fresh cut blaze BLEEDS in spring, because birch and maple genuinely run sap, so a cut made minutes ago is wet.** | Cut blazing wounds trees and is no longer acceptable practice on public land, which is perfect for us: **a fresh hatchet blaze in the present day is a transgression.** |
| **Cairn** [VERIFIED] | A deliberate pile of rock used where there are no trees: above treeline, on bare slab, on scree, across a boulder field. Conical or columnar, sized to be seen from the previous cairn. Two or three stones is a "duck." | The cairn is a blaze made of the only material available, so its size is a function of visibility distance. | **A cairn in a forested act is WRONG unless the trail crosses bare rock, which is exactly the fly rod on the pond.** Recreational cairn building is actively discouraged by trail organisations because it creates FALSE TRAILS, so a cairn where none is needed is not charming, it is a hazard, and hikers know it. **A cairn built of stone that had to be CARRIED IN, in a place that needs no cairn, is a real observation a hiker would make and a genuinely unsettling one.** |
| **THE TREAD ITSELF, which nobody draws** [VERIFIED] | A real trail is not a line, it is a TROUGH. Thirty to sixty centimetres wide, worn BELOW the surrounding duff. Tree roots cross it at right angles, polished smooth and pale on top. Rocks in it are worn shiny. No leaf litter in the middle, litter piled at the edges. Where it gets muddy, people walk around, so the tread BRAIDS into two or three parallel paths. Switchback corners are cut. The plants at the edge are different, tougher species. | Wear is heaviest where routes converge and vanishes where nobody walks. | A path that runs dead straight into a door is the tell. |

### THE EYE-IN-RING HIJACK, WHICH ONLY WORKS IF THE MARKS ARE COMPETENT FIRST [VERIFIED as a design principle]

The horror is not the symbol. **It is the correctness.** Trail marking is a system of pure
inherited trust: you follow marks made by people you will never meet, in a place you do not
know, and you have to, because the alternative is being lost. Nothing else in ordinary life
demands that much faith in an anonymous stranger's competence.

**GET ALL SIX RIGHT FIRST:** right size (a two by six inch rectangle, so roughly one by three
at portrait scale), right height (eye level, about one and a half to one and three quarter
metres), right trees (the ones you naturally look at, facing the walker), right spacing (from
one you can see the next), right tool (a hatchet cut to sapwood, with the callus roll if it is
old), right two sidedness. **Then, and only then, betray:**

| Betrayal | What it does |
|---|---|
| A double blaze that turns you off the tread | A hundred metres later the tread is not under your feet any more. |
| Blazes on the walker facing side ONLY | The route works in one direction and you cannot walk out the way you came in. |
| A blaze the tree has grown around | It was cut decades ago somewhere nobody has walked in decades. |
| Blazes continuing past where any real trail would end | |
| **Every pupil oriented consistently** | They are all looking at the same point in the woods, and following the gaze is the puzzle. |
| Sap still wet in the cut | Whoever cut it is still nearby. |

And the counter image: **Hearthwood's own honest old blazes, closed to dark lens scars,
running the other way.**

### THE BEST ONE IN THE DOCUMENT [VERIFIED]

**SONGBIRDS MOB PREDATORS.** A knot of chickadees, nuthatches and jays scolding loudly in one
spot tells you exactly where an owl is roosting, from a hundred metres, without you seeing
anything. Birds calling attention to a thing you cannot see is free, accurate, and precisely
what this game is about.

And the reverse: a woods that goes completely silent.

## 6.2 TEENAGE PROPORTION AND HOW GEAR SITS ON IT [VERIFIED, and already law in ART_STYLE.md]

A small child is about five to five and a half heads tall with a large cranium and almost no
neck. A teenager is about six and a half to seven, with a visible neck, real shoulders, and
hands and feet already at nearly adult size while the limbs are still catching up, so wrists
and ankles read large.

**It matters here because it changes how every piece of gear behaves.** A child has no iliac
crest to speak of, so a pack hipbelt has nothing to sit on and the whole load hangs from the
shoulders, which is why a pack on a child rides high and looks borrowed. A teenager has hips,
so the hipbelt sits ON TOP of the crest and carries most of the weight, and **the shoulder
straps go SLACK.** That slack is the visual difference, and it is the fastest way to say
"old enough to be sent."

Three checks: **POSTURE** (weight on one leg, hips cocked, one shoulder lower, a slouch a
scoutmaster would correct); **LOAD PATH** (on a fitted pack the load lifter straps run at
roughly 45 degrees from the shoulder straps up to the frame; flat or downhill means the pack
is the wrong size, which is right for a scavenged pack and wrong for the player's own);
**SLEEVES** (a teenager pushes them up in fat uneven turns above the elbow; a child's sleeves
are simply too long).

## 6.3 PACKS

| | EXTERNAL FRAME (Troop 12's era) | INTERNAL FRAME (the player's era) |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | A ladder of aluminium tubing with two vertical stays and three or four cross members. The bag is bolted or CLEVIS PINNED on. Two or three nylon BACKBANDS laced across the frame, and those, not the bag, are what your back rests against. The frame extends above and below the bag, giving a shelf and a lash bar. | Two flat aluminium stays or a moulded framesheet in a sleeve, bent to follow the spine. One main body with a lid, side compression straps, a padded hipbelt with wings, load lifters, a sternum strap on rails. |
| **Why** | The bag never touches your back, so air moves and you do not soak through. Compression straps pull the load IN toward your back, which is their actual job: left loose, the pack sags away and pulls you backwards. |
| **Right if** | **The clevis pins.** Every used external frame has at least one missing, replaced with a bent nail, a split ring off a keyring, or wire. The anodising rubs off exactly where the shoulder strap crosses, leaving a bright bare band. Grommets tear out at the TOP corners first. It can STAND UP by itself against a tree, which an internal frame cannot, and that is itself a tell. It RATTLES, always. | **Buckle vocabulary.** SIDE RELEASE (the plastic squeeze clip) on hipbelts, sternum straps and lid straps. | **Abandoned** | See 4.6: a standing frame and two curved rows of grommets. | Propped upright against a rock, lid open, hipbelt still buckled. A buckled hipbelt on a grounded pack means it was not taken off, it was got OUT OF. |

**THE ERA SIGNAL, and it is the cheapest one in the document:** give Troop 12 external frames
and the player's scouts internal frames. Anyone who has carried a pack reads thirty years off
the frame type without a word of text, and everyone else just sees two different packs.

**THE BEDROLL** [VERIFIED]: rolled TIGHT from one end and tied at **THREE** points, both ends
and the middle. Two ties makes a roll that sags and works loose in a mile. The first turn is
the one that matters: slack first turn means the whole roll is slack. Wrapped in a groundsheet
or poncho with the ends folded in. Tied with a CONSTRICTOR or a clove hitch backed with two
half hitches, because those grip a cylinder. **It goes on the BOTTOM of the pack.** On top makes
the load top heavy and catches every branch. Abandoned: half rolled, blanket flat, mat under
it, one end started and the roll opened back out. Somebody was packing to leave and stopped.

**WHAT HANGS ON THE OUTSIDE** [VERIFIED]: things go outside if you need them without stopping,
if they are wet, or if they stink. Correct: a water bottle CAP FACING OUT so it can be got at
one handed; wet socks under a compression strap; a foam sit pad under the lid straps; a trowel
in its own pocket handle up; a whistle on the sternum strap; poles or a staff under the side
straps **TIPS DOWN**, because tips up is how you take out the eye of the person behind you. And
**a HATCHET goes HEAD DOWN and handle up, low against the frame,** because a hatchet head at
shoulder height is a hatchet that meets your skull when you fall.

**Check what is NOT outside:** food (bears and everything smaller), the first aid kit (it must
stay dry and outside things get torn off in brush), the map, anything electronic. A pack with
the food bag bungeed to the lid was drawn by somebody who has not camped.

**THE CHARACTER READ, which is worth a whole table of prop variants:**

| Pack | Says |
|---|---|
| Everything symmetrical, tails rolled, nothing swinging, one small foam pad | An experienced kid, or a kid whose adult packed it and checked it. |
| Everything clipped on and jingling: a mug, a mascot, a dozen carabiners | A kid who is excited and has not walked far yet. |
| Half empty with the compression straps cranked flat | Somebody who left in a hurry, or somebody carrying out what is left of another person's load. |
| One shoulder strap replaced in mismatched webbing and hand sewn | This pack has been repaired, which means it is either loved or it is all there is. |

## 6.4 MATERIALS

### ONE RULE THAT REPLACES FIVE SEPARATE FINDINGS

> **NOTHING AGES UNIFORMLY, AND THE PART THAT DID NOT AGE IS ALWAYS THE PART A HAND OR A FLAP PROTECTED.**

That generalises to every material we will ever paint. Everything below is a worked example of it.

| Material | Behaves like [VERIFIED unless marked] | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Waxed canvas** | Cotton duck impregnated with paraffin and beeswax: heavy, water shedding, barely breathable. | **The wax MIGRATES AWAY from wear points, so corners, fold lines and strap contacts go PALE while flat panels stay dark.** Pale creases on dark cloth is the signature and it is instantly recognisable. Near a fire the wax softens and takes ash and grit into itself. |
| **Webbing** | Nylon (strong, slightly stretchy, glossy), polyester (less stretch, far better in sun), cotton (older gear, soft, rots). | Nylon degrades badly in UV and **the frightening part is that it looks fine while it does,** which is why climbers retire slings by age. Faded colour is the visible warning. A cut end is either a hard shiny heat sealed bead or a fringe, and a frayed unsealed end means somebody cut it with a knife and had no lighter. |
| **Leather** | Vegetable tanned (stiff, pale tan new, darkens with oil and sweat and sun into a deep patina, remembers every scratch) for belts, sheaths, slides. Chrome tanned (softer, coloured through) for most boots. | **The high points that hands touch stay LIGHT AND POLISHED and the recesses go DARK, which is the inverse of how people usually shade leather.** Wet leather dried fast by a fire shrinks and goes hard, permanently. [Do NOT use "red rot": it is a real conservation term but is documented in heated polluted indoor environments, not damp forests.] |
| **Wool** | Crimped scaled fibre that insulates when wet because the crimp holds air even soaked. | **WOOL DOES NOT READILY BURN. It chars, self extinguishes, and smells like burnt hair. Synthetic fleece MELTS, and melted synthetic sticks to skin.** That is the real reason the blanket around the shoulders at a campfire is wool, and it is very drawable camp knowledge. Wool PILLS at friction points and FELTS where it was rubbed while wet. **Thirty years indoors is not rot: moths and carpet beetles eat keratin, so a stored blanket has a scatter of clean edged ROUND HOLES, a dusting of frass, and is otherwise intact. Draw a moth eaten blanket, not a rotted one.** |
| **Rubber and foam** | Oxidises, hardens, cracks first where it was stretched. | Elastic is the first thing in any old kit to fail. **Polyurethane foam turns to ORANGE DUST, so old pack shoulder pads are empty fabric sleeves full of orange crumbs.** Polyurethane boot midsoles HYDROLYSE: they look perfectly fine and then crumble to gravel at a touch. |
| **Enamelware** | Steel dipped in powdered glass and fired: a glass barrier that does not corrode at all, but CHIPS. | **The rim is a band of a contrasting colour, classically black, because the glass is thinnest at the rim.** Chips are sharp edged craters with a pale ring and a dark centre, each blooming an orange rust halo. **A white enamel plate covered in orange rimmed black chips is the single most recognisable camp object there is.** And enamel conducts straight to your lip, so a full mug of cocoa burns your mouth and everyone who used one flinched once. |
| **Tinplate** | Thin mild steel with a very thin tin coating. A barrier that does NOT protect once scratched, so it rusts from every scratch outward. | **Tin is soft, takes a stamp beautifully, and CORROSION COLLECTS IN THE RECESSES, so a thirty year old stamped tin token is grey brown and its impression reads HARDER at a distance than when it was new.** Our Sign token lore is materially correct. **Do NOT use "tin pest":** it sounds perfect, and it needs sustained cold and applies to pure tin, and tinplate is mostly steel. |
| **Galvanised steel** | Steel dipped in molten zinc. Zinc is SACRIFICIAL, corroding in preference to the steel, which is the exact opposite of tinplate. | New hot dip has SPANGLE, a crystalline flower pattern of irregular facets catching light differently, fading within a year or two to matte grey. White powdery zinc corrosion appears where two galvanised things touch and hold water. Rust holes only in the bottom and at the seams where it stood in water. |
| **Brass** | Does not rust. | **Dark in the recesses and gleaming on every edge a hand touches.** That contrast is the look and it says handled for years. [Do NOT use dezincification: it is real in some plumbing water and is not what happens to a buckle in leaf litter.] |
| **Lantern glass and mantles** | A mantle is a cloth sock impregnated with metal salts: you tie it on and BURN IT OFF, leaving a fragile white ash skeleton that glows brilliantly and that a single knock destroys. | **A lantern in a pack is a lantern with a broken mantle.** The globe goes cloudy with soot from the inside and gets a permanent brown ring at the top where the flame sits. The top hat heat blues around the vents. The pump plunger on an old lantern is a LEATHER CUP that dries out and stops sealing, and the fix is a drop of oil down the pump shaft, which is genuine gear knowledge a character could say. |
| **Alkaline cells** | Vent potassium hydroxide over decades. | **THE BEST THIRTY YEAR PROP IN THE GAME:** a 1996 torch found today does not work and cannot be made to work, because the leaked electrolyte crystallises into a white green crusty bloom that WELDS the cells into the tube and corrodes the spring to green powder. Shake it and it does not rattle. Everyone who has opened an old drawer has seen it, and it is a colour we do not otherwise use. |

## 7.1 FISHING: THE OWNER'S OWN EXAMPLE, AND THE CORRECTION THAT MAKES THE RULE SHARPER

**THE RULE AS ORIGINALLY STATED is very nearly right and is worth stating precisely, because
the whole point of this document is that it is correct.**

Fly fishing STILL water is a real and old discipline: stillwater trout on lakes, and fly rod
bass bugging on ponds, which is one of the oldest American fly traditions there is. **So a fly
rod on a pond is not automatically wrong.**

> **WHAT IS WRONG IS A TROUT STREAM KIT ON A CAMP POND. The tell is not fly versus spin. It is STREAM KIT versus POND KIT, and the giveaway objects are the WADERS and the NET.**

The rule survives intact and gets sharper. Chest waders, a wading staff, a stream vest with fly
boxes, a wooden framed rubber bagged landing net, tippet spools, floatant and a nine foot five
weight say COLD MOVING WATER and a person who drove there ALONE to do a specific thing. Put
that on our camp bank and every angler alive clocks it, **and in our fiction an adult is
already a loaded thing for a prop to say.**

| | FLY, MOVING WATER [VERIFIED] | SPIN, STILL WATER [VERIFIED] |
|---|---|---|
| **The one fact that generates everything** | **The LINE is the casting weight and the fly is essentially weightless.** That is the reverse of every other kind of fishing. | **The WEIGHT is at the business end** and the line is thin. The lure or the shot pulls line off the spool. |
| **Rod** | Eight to nine feet or more, thin, two to four sections, cork grip, and **the REEL SEAT IS BELOW THE GRIP so the reel hangs UNDER the rod at the very bottom.** One large stripping guide nearest the reel, then small snake guides. Long, because you have to move a heavy line. **In tight brook trout cover: SHORT, seven to eight feet, three or four weight, because you roll cast.** | Five to six and a half feet, thicker, fewer larger guides, reel seat clamping the reel in the MIDDLE of the handle. Short and thick because you are casting a weight. |
| **Reel** | A wide thin EXPOSED spool, essentially a line holder, with a drag knob. Big and exposed so the thick line coils without memory. | SPINCAST (closed face, push button, the classic first rod, which exists so a child can cast without a backlash) or SPINNING (open face, bail arm). Both hang UNDER the rod. |
| **Line** | THICK, brightly coloured, plastic coated, then a tapered LEADER, then TIPPET, which is what you replace as you change flies. The taper is what lays the fly down softly instead of slapping it. | Monofilament, clear or low vis green, one weight all the way through, no leader system. |
| **Terminal** | Flies: dry (floats in the surface film), nymph (sinks), streamer (baitfish), tied from thread, feather and fur, carried in a FLY BOX with foam slits. | A bait hook, two or three SPLIT SHOT pinched eight to twelve inches above the hook, and a BOBBER. The bobber does three jobs: it holds the bait at a chosen depth above the weed, it registers a bite you would never feel, and it stops the bait sinking. |
| **Worn or carried** | **EVERYTHING ON THE BODY, because you are standing in current and cannot put anything down.** A vest or sling covered in pockets: nippers on a retractable zinger, forceps, floatant, split shot, tippet spools, and a FLY PATCH on the chest where used wet flies are stuck to dry. A wide shallow NET on a magnetic release at the back. Waders, wading boots, a staff, polarised glasses (not vanity: they cut glare so you can see the fish and the rocks you are about to step on). | **A TACKLE BOX ON THE GROUND, open, trays fanned in a stepped arc.** Pliers or a disgorger, a stringer or bucket, a rag, and something to SIT ON. |
| **The rod holder** | **There is none. You cannot leave a fly rod in a holder; the whole method is continuous.** | **A FORKED STICK pushed into the bank, a pipe in the sand, or a holder screwed to a dock rail.** This is the loudest possible signal of which discipline this is. |
| **Where** | A river, a stream, a creek with riffle and pool. The angler works UPSTREAM and across and lets the fly drift back down, because trout hold facing INTO the current behind rocks and in seams. | A lake, pond, slow bend, dock, jon boat. **It points at STRUCTURE:** weed edges, sunken timber, dock pilings, drop-offs. A correct scene has the rod aimed at a FEATURE, not at open water. |
| **Plainly wrong** | A spinning or spincast reel on a fly rod. The reel mounted ABOVE the grip. A bobber. A bait bucket. Worms. A treble hooked plug. A tackle box. A rod holder. Stagnant water with lily pads. | A fly reel on a spinning rod. A spinning reel mounted on TOP (that is a baitcaster, a different and more advanced reel, plausible on an adult but not on a first rod). Waders on a dock. A wading staff on flat water. **The absolute howler: a bobber and a tub of worms next to a fly vest and a nine foot rod.** |
| **ABANDONED** | The rod on the bank, butt on the gravel, tip out over the water, the line running into the current with a big unmended downstream belly and the fly dragging. **THE ROD TIP IS STILL BENT, because the current is still pulling on the line.** That single bend says the water has not stopped and the person has. The vest on a branch with the zinger dangling, the net in the water hooked on nothing, floatant open in the gravel, and one fly stuck in the bank grass behind where a person stood, from a backcast that went high. | The rod in the forked stick, line out, **and the BOBBER SITTING UP ON THE WATER, undisturbed.** Nobody has touched this and nothing has bitten. The tackle box open with one tray tipped into the grass, the bait tub on its side with the soil out, one folding chair empty facing the water. **THE WORSE VERSION, one bent line: the bobber is UNDER the water and the rod is bent, and whatever is on the line has been on it a long time.** [An angler also reads a bobber lying FLAT rather than standing as meaning the weight is not hanging any more, which means snagged or bait gone, which means a very long time. One rotation.] |

# 8. WEARING IT WRONG

## 8.1 THE TWO FAULTS

Signed critters wear SCAVENGED scout gear and the Mark makes them imitate without
understanding. That produces **two separate faults**, and the best ones compound both.

| Fault | What it is | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **FIT** | The gear was made for a human teenager, so on a small animal it is comically oversized and drags, and on a large one it cuts in and cannot close. | The cheaper and more visible of the two, and already law: if a Signed critter's gear looks tailored to it, you have painted a mascot. |
| **COMPREHENSION** | The item is used for its SHAPE and not its FUNCTION. | **This is where the horror actually lives.** A thing that copies FUNCTION is a mimic, and mimics are ordinary. A thing that copies APPEARANCE is something that WATCHED FROM A DISTANCE and never got close enough to learn what anything was for. Every best item below is a shape copied without its purpose, and that is a statement about how far away the watcher was and for how long. |

## 8.2 THE NECKERCHIEF IS A THREE WAY, NOT A TWO WAY

**Research proposed a two way (scouts correct, Signed wrong). ART_STYLE.md already carries a
better THREE way and it wins.** Flattening it would lose the best part.

| Who | How they wear it | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **BROCK, and any adult who was taught** | Fully correct: rolled, point down the back, two tails, slide at the throat. **He is the REFERENCE.** | The system, working. |
| **MAX, JUNIE, GUS** | Rolled correctly but worn LOOSE and shoved sideways. Slightly wrong ON PURPOSE, the way a teenager wears anything. | Old enough to know and old enough not to care, which is the age the whole story is about. |
| **THE SIGNED** | The BIB: a triangle hanging point down the chest, wide edge at the neck, which is precisely backwards. | Something that saw a photograph. |

At 40 px these are three different silhouettes: a tidy ring with two tails, a slumped
asymmetric band, and a hanging triangle. **The correction to js/sprites.js:1543 (see 9.1) is
what buys all three, and it converts the single most common mistake in scouting art into the
game's clearest piece of storytelling.**

## 8.3 THE CATALOGUE OF WRONGNESS, RANKED BY 40 PX LEGIBILITY

**One clear wrongness per critter, never a pile.** One accent per creature already holds and
the same logic applies here.

### READS AT 40 PX. USE THESE ON TRASH CRITTERS.

| # | The error | Why it lands |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The neckerchief worn as a BIB.** | See 8.2. Exact opposite of ours at any scale. |
| 2 | **The pack on a limb that cannot carry it.** Both straps over one ear, the hipbelt buckled around a neck, one shoulder strap around a hind leg so it drags. | Worse with one addition: **the pack is FULL and its compression straps are cranked tight,** which means it was PACKED, carefully, by something that understands a pack should be full and does not understand what to put in it. |
| 3 | **The hat on PERFECTLY STRAIGHT.** Brim exactly level, front exactly forward, no crease, no sweat line. | **The best in the list, and it is counterintuitive: it is not damage, it is the ABSENCE OF HABIT.** A person wears a hat at an angle, pushed back or tipped down, shaped over a season. A hat set exactly level and exactly forward has been PUT ON by something following a diagram. Nothing is torn, nothing is bloody, and it is deeply wrong. |
| 4 | **Half a shirt.** | Silhouette break. |
| 5 | **The badge sash worn as a GIRTH,** around the barrel of the body or the throat instead of over one shoulder to the opposite hip. | The diagonal is the read; a horizontal band is not it. |
| 6 | **Boots on the wrong feet, or ONE boot, laced, on a paw with no heel.** | |
| 7 | **The neckerchief knotted where a human COLLAR would be, on a body where that spot is the middle of the back,** so a bat wears it as a cape between the wings. | |

### PORTRAIT AND FIELD GUIDE TIER ONLY. Do not spend these at 40 px.

| # | The error | Why it lands |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | **The shirt buttoned OFFSET BY ONE,** or only at the top, or with limbs through the wrong holes. | **The most human error there is, which is exactly what makes it disquieting on an animal: it means something TRIED, in order, and got it slightly wrong.** |
| 9 | **An item CARRIED that should be WORN and one WORN that should be CARRIED.** A hatchet sheath on a belt with a stick in it. A canteen cover full of stones. | **The purest expression of shape over function and arguably scarier than anything torn.** |
| 10 | **The slide worn WITHOUT the neckerchief:** a wooden ring on an ear, a tail, a horn, a tusk. | |
| 11 | **Whistle, lanyard and compass all worn and none used:** the whistle hanging behind the head, the compass face down. | |
| 12 | **Every webbing tail at full length, uncut, unrolled, hanging.** | |
| 13 | **The gear CLEAN and the animal FILTHY.** A neckerchief rolled and pressed on something with mud to the knees. | |

**THE FIT RULE THAT MAKES ALL THIRTEEN WORK:** the gear is sized for a human TEENAGER. On a
squirrel it swamps and drags; on a stag it cuts in and cannot close. Both are correct. **Gear
that fits is forbidden.**

**WHY THIS SECTION EXISTS AT ALL:** if an artist cannot state the RIGHT version of an error
they are painting, they will paint a generic mess instead of a specific wrongness, and a
generic mess reads as bad art rather than as horror. Every item above is defined against a
specific right documented in sections 3 through 7.

## 8.4 THE ONE THAT IS WORN RIGHT

**Once. Not twice.**

One critter in the entire roster wears its gear PERFECTLY CORRECTLY. Neckerchief rolled, point
down the back, slide at the throat. Hat creased, with a sweat line. Boots laced eyelets then
hooks and double knotted. Pack fitted, hipbelt on the hips, load lifters at 45 degrees, webbing
tails rolled and held with a keeper. Nothing oversized, nothing dragging, nothing offset.

**Every check in this document passes. That IS the test, and it is why this entry cannot exist
without the other thirty.**

Because every other Signed thing is wrong in a way that says it copied from a distance, the one
that is right says something categorically different: **it did not copy. It learned, or it was
taught, or it used to be somebody.** The player will not be able to say what is wrong with it,
because nothing is. And the moment they see it, every wrongly worn item they already walked
past reclassifies from art style into evidence.

**FLAGGED AS A SUGGESTION, NOT SETTLED LORE, because this touches story.** There is an obvious
hook already in the build: a tin nameplate sanded blank with the scratches still bright, a sash
of eleven badges with a twelfth torn off, and eleven names in a scoutmaster's block print with a
twelfth line in a child's handwriting (all in js/finds.js). Whatever the twelfth name turns out
to be is the natural owner of correctly worn gear. **Take that to whoever owns STORY_DESIGN.md
rather than treating it as decided.**

## 10.8 THE FIELD GUIDE: REAL NATURAL HISTORY IS WHAT MAKES THE LYING LEGIBLE

**THE THREE LINES, always in this order:**

1. **What it is**, in cold field guide register, and CORRECT.
2. **How you know it is near** (the real sign, checkable natural history).
3. **The tell that keeps you alive** (combat).

Lines 1 and 2 are true. Line 3 is the game.

**THE UNRELIABILITY LIVES IN THE MARGIN, NOT IN THE ENTRIES. This is the whole design.** The
printed plates are accurate. The PENCILLED ANNOTATIONS are where the book lies: dates that do
not agree with each other, a species he says he has never seen with a worn thumb mark on its
plate, an entry crossed out and rewritten.

> **A guide that is right about every animal and wrong about the year is evidence against its
> author PRECISELY BECAUSE everything else in it checks out. If the natural history were wrong
> too, the player would read the whole book as unreliable and nothing would land. Accuracy is
> what buys the betrayal.**

**Three hands on the page:** printed plate text, Brock's block capitals, and in exactly one
entry a child's handwriting. Same mechanic as the roll book, the sash and the nameplate.

**The plate count becomes evidence:** the printed line says the guide was printed with N plates,
and there are N+1 plates in the book. One was added by hand.

**The Signed get plates too, and their plates are the tell:** the same register applied to a
thing with no natural history, so line 2 is the only line that can be filled in, and lines 1
and 3 are blank ruled paper.

**And the real content is already written.** The ten `sign_*` finds each carry a correct tell
and an instruction. **Those ARE line 2 and line 3.** Eighteen species entries at three lines
each is the cheapest large block in the game and half of it is already in js/finds.js.

# 11. THE TRADEMARK LINE

**Stated once, clearly. Practical notes, not legal advice.** Scouting America (formerly the Boy
Scouts of America) holds trademarks on its name, emblems, uniform insignia and merit badge
artwork, with a federal charter behind them (36 U.S.C. ch. 309). A charter is a materially
stronger instrument than ordinary trademark law and it reaches WORDS as adopted.

## 11.1 THE ONE THAT IS BEING UNDER-RAISED

> **"MERIT BADGE" IN THE TITLE IS NOT A FOOTNOTE QUESTION.**

Two research passes mentioned it and both punted. That is the right instinct and the wrong
volume. The phrase sits in the largest type on the Steam page of a PAID product, and the
federal charter above is not defeated by the usual descriptive-use arguments as easily as an
ordinary mark would be.

**I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice.** What I am confident of is that this is a
**one real opinion question, it should be answered BEFORE art and marketing are built on the
title, and answering it after launch is the expensive order.** It is outside a props audit and
it would be negligent to leave it at the size the research left it. **Section 12, item 1.**

## 11.2 THE ENSEMBLE, WHICH NOBODY FLAGGED

Every research pass checked items ONE AT A TIME and cleared each one. The broad brim four dent
felt hat is generic (rangers, drill instructors, Baden-Powell himself). Knee socks are generic.
Epaulettes, neckerchief and slide, sash, and a khaki button front shirt with box pleat pockets
are all generic.

**Assemble all six on one figure and you have drawn a specific organisation's uniform without
using a single emblem. Trade dress is its own category and it does not care that the parts are
individually old.**

**RECOMMENDATION, and it turns the constraint into a story asset:**

| Change | Cost | Gain |
|---|---|---|
| **COLOUR.** Hearthwood green over slate, or rust over grey. Anything that is not khaki-and-olive. | Palette values. | The silhouette becomes ours. |
| **OUR SASH IS NOT A SASH. Make it a BANDOLIER OF TIN TOKENS on a strap.** | One painter. | Already the game's own lore object, reads instantly at 40 px, unmistakably not a merit badge sash, and it makes the trophy the same material as the threat. |

## PROP COHERENCE (AUTHENTICITY.md, binding)

A prop is not one object. It is a SET of objects that must agree with each other and
with the ground they sit on. A fly rod brings a fly reel, a vest, tippet, a net and
MOVING water. A spinning rod brings a bobber, worms, a tackle box and STILL water. Put
the stream kit on a pond and every angler clocks it, and nobody who does not fish gains
anything from the mistake either.

CORRECT COSTS EXACTLY THE SAME AS WRONG. Three graded lean sticks with a gap on one side
is the same number of draw calls as two crossed logs. One of them is a fire lay.

THE ACCEPTANCE TEST, run it on every prop and every kit you make:
  Could someone who actually does this thing tell what happened here?
  Name that person out loud: the angler, the pioneering instructor, the waterfront
  director, the forester. If you cannot name who would check it, you are painting
  texture, not a prop, and texture does not get to claim this rule.

AND EVERY SET PIECE IS AN INTERRUPTED MOMENT, never an arrangement of objects. A correct
prop also says WHO LEFT IT AND WHAT THEY WERE DOING WHEN THEY STOPPED. A rod on the bank
with the line still out and the tip still bent says the water has not stopped and the
person has. Design every kit with its abandoned state, and AUTHENTICITY.md gives you one
for every entry.

THIS SERVES THE GORE TOGGLE RATHER THAN COMPETING WITH IT. Almost nothing in
AUTHENTICITY.md needs blood. An opened first aid kit does the whole job of a blood pool
and is more specific, because a blood pool says something happened and an opened kit says
somebody TRIED.

THE SCALE RULE STILL OUTRANKS ACCURACY. AUTHENTICITY.md section 9.4 lists which correct
details cannot resolve at 40 px (box pleats, garter tabs, frapping turns, whipped rope
ends, a worn knife edge). Those are portrait, camp and find-card tier. A correct detail
that turns to mud at game scale is still mud.

CONFIDENCE TAGS ARE BINDING TOO. Entries marked [VERIFIED] you may draw and a character
may state. Entries marked [LIKELY] you may draw, and no character states a number from
them as gospel. Entries marked [NEEDS A HUMAN] do not go on screen; raise them in your
report instead.

## THE GROUND HAS TO AGREE WITH ITSELF (AUTHENTICITY.md, binding)

A room is a SET, not a list of props. Terrain, dressing and cast must agree with each
other and with the act they are in, and the check is: could somebody who works in these
woods tell what this place is? Three worked failures currently in the game, all of them

plausible scale, and every real camp separation is longer than it is wide. A person who
knows camps does not measure your pixels; they check the SEQUENCE. Is the latrine
furthest from the water and downhill of the tents. Is the fire between the tents and the
trail rather than upwind of the tents. Is the food store away from where people sleep.
Get the order right and the compression is invisible.

THREE TOOLS THAT BUY A ZONE YOU HAVE NO ROOM FOR: a path that LEAVES THE BOUNDS with a

MS Paint."
